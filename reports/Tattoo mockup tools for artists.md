# Show live size on real bodies

Most tattoo artists still preview designs by laying them over a phone photo of the client's body in Procreate, sending the mockup by DM, and checking it against a paper stencil on the day. They quote by **estimated hours, not square inches**, with the price floored at a shop minimum. The size number on most request forms is the client's guess, and that guess is the weakest part of the process. The tools on the market split into AR and AI photo overlays that look like stickers and give no real scale, and a few 3D body viewers and Procreate model packs with **one to four fixed body presets** and no measured size readout. No tool found combines true-to-height scale, continuous body-shape sliders and distortion-free wrap; our app already does all three. The changes that matter most for a solo artist are small and close at hand. First, show a **live W × H label in inches and cm on the design itself while she stretches it**. Second, add Anny's own linear belly, thigh, hip and bust morphs, which can be baked as exact extra morph targets the same way the height/build/muscle grid is today. Third, generate a consult card (size, zone, style, flags) that she prices with her own hourly rate. Skin texture (wrinkles, stretch marks, healed or aged ink) is worth doing, but it costs more and the evidence for it is weaker, so it belongs in a later phase. Caveat on sources: nearly all evidence below comes from search-result summaries of studio, vendor and SEO blog pages. Reddit and most primary pages were blocked, and no study measures what tattoo-specific preview tools do for clients.

## Artists mock up on photos and quote by the hour

The working artist's toolkit is an iPad. "The vast majority of tattoo artists design ... on an iPad with ... Procreate", and many show "a preview photo of the tattooed body part" built from nothing more than a picture of the customer ([Medium / Starr Tattoo Supplies](https://medium.com/starr-tattoo-supplies/how-to-design-your-tattoo-on-procreate-38952429d4b0)). The artist puts the body photo on a low-opacity layer, places the design above it, and resizes and rotates it into position ([Envato Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-use-procreate-for-tattoos--cms-109190)). She can then "send clients a visual mockup before they come in, which clients love" ([Myclaw](https://myclaw.ai/blog/procreate-tattoo-design-app)). When no photo exists, or to show how a design wraps a limb, artists buy Procreate 3D body packs. Sellers say these show "exactly where a stencil would warp around the bicep and forearm" ([bebrushes summary](https://bebrushes.com/best-3d-models-for-procreate/)). Final approval still happens on the body: the printed stencil is "the last easy moment to change placement, size or angle" ([Art n Soul](https://artnsoul.net.au/how-to-choose-tattoo-size-and-placement/)).

There is a skeptical view, and it shows where a tool earns trust. A Los Angeles shop calls placement mockup apps "almost universally misleading" because they ignore how skin folds and moves and are viewed at "phone-camera-distance". Its alternative is tracing paper held on the forearm in a mirror ([losangelestattooshop.com](https://losangelestattooshop.com/ai-and-tattoos/)). That complaint is about **scale and skin behaviour, not render polish**. A 3D tool that measures honestly answers it; a prettier AI composite does not.

Pricing has four parts. Studios charge a **shop minimum of about $100–$200**, flat per-piece prices for small work, an **hourly rate of roughly $150–$250 for established US artists** (from $80–$130 in small markets up to $300–$400+ for top city artists), and half-day or full-day session rates for large work ([Tattoo Studio Pro](https://tattoostudiopro.com/tattoo-prices/); [Certified Tattoo Academy](https://www.certifiedtattooacademy.com/blog/how-much-does-a-tattoo-artist-charge)). Real price lists confirm the pattern. Fatty's Tattoos charges at least $200/hr for 7+ hour pieces, prices shorter work by the piece, and raises its minimum from $100 to **$150 for head, face, neck, elbows, knees, hands and feet** ([Fatty's](https://www.fattystattoos.com/tattoo-pricing)). Studio City Tattoo has a $120 minimum and a $1,000 six-hour day ([Studio City Tattoo](https://www.studiocitytattoo.com/tattoo-deposit-page/)). The factor that ties these together is time: "Most quality artists don't price by the square inch. They price by time" ([Tattoo Studio Pro](https://tattoostudiopro.com/tattoo-cost-by-size/)). Size, style (realism costs more than color, color more than black-and-grey, black-and-grey more than linework), placement difficulty (ribs about **20–30% more** than the same design on an arm), cover-ups and scars all change the price because they add hours or sessions ([Tattoo Studio Pro, by body part](https://tattoostudiopro.com/tattoo-cost-by-body-part/)). The size-to-price charts in guides disagree by 2× or more, so they are only rough anchors.

To quote, artists collect the same core fields almost everywhere: contact details and age, idea and style, **exact placement**, **size in inches**, color vs black-and-grey, reference images, cover-up or nearby tattoos, budget and availability. A non-refundable deposit of about $100–$300 follows approval. Monolith, Studio OUCH!, Cozy and CTRL+ALT+Z all ask for size and placement, and CTRL+ALT+Z also asks for skin type ([Monolith](https://monolithstudio.com/faq); [Studio OUCH!](https://www.ouch.studio/tattoos/booking); [Cozy](https://cozytattoostudio.com/policies); [CTRL+ALT+Z](https://ctrlaltz.tattoo/information/)). Some artists also want a photo of the body area "for flow, proportion, and sizing" ([Minh Pham Tattoo](https://minhphamtattoo.com/blog/how-to-message-a-tattoo-artist/)). Booking platforms such as Porter, Venue, Tattoodo Books and Vagaro run the request → approve/quote → deposit → schedule pipeline. None of the ones found includes a body mockup ([Porter](https://www.getporter.io/features/online-booking); [Tattoodo Books](https://www.tattoodo.com/books)). For our tool, the useful output is therefore not a dollar figure. It is an **objective size and placement spec**, which replaces the client's guess, plus a slot where the artist enters her own hours and rate.

## Competitors offer presets and stickers, not measured bodies

The market falls into four groups, and none of them covers what a consultation needs. **Live-camera AR** (InkHunter, Inkjin, Tattoodo's reported 2025 "try it on") tracks a marker or the skin. InkHunter's overlay "would remain flat and float off of the arm" at the limb's edge and loses tracking when the camera moves ([Medium XR evaluation](https://medium.com/@pchang4/xr-evaluation-1-inkhunter-79d9ad8d808c)). **AI photo try-on** (Tatship, Fotor, PreInk and many clones) blends a design into a photo. Tatship scored **2/5 for "quality results"** in one aggregated review ([nemovideo](https://www.nemovideo.com/alternative/tatship)), and reviewers of overlay apps describe the result as "literally a sticker" ([Google Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mirouu.tatoyourself&hl=en)). **3D body viewers** include TryTattoo3D on the web (free; $4.99 one-time for your own uploads and custom skin tone; $4.99/month to save, share or export) and an iPad app, "Tattoo Preview – 3D & AR". Neither listing mentions measured sizes or shape sliders ([TryTattoo3D pricing](https://trytattoo3d.com/pricing/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tattoo-preview-3d-ar/id6760173684)). **Artist-side kits** are Procreate model packs and Photoshop displacement-map mockups.

The complaints in reviews point to the same opening. Users object to weekly subscriptions and upsell pop-ups ("a popup every few minutes trying to get you pay $6 a week"). They object to the **removal of custom upload** in favor of AI designs, which reviewers "widely criticized" ([Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/intel-report/lifestyle/inkhunter-inkhunter-pro)). And they object to designs that do not follow the body. Skin tone is the one customization almost everyone offers. Continuous belly, thigh or bust control was not found in any tattoo tool, and no tool was found that simulates healed or aged ink or exports a stencil. The app's foundations already match the evidence: its Phase 1 measurements show the surface wrap keeps **100% of a 5 in forearm design within 5% of true size** where a cylinder manages 34%, and it solves for the client's height instead of scaling the body ([Phase 1 report](https://github.com/samike93/Tattoo)). The gap is in how the app presents those numbers to the artist and client, not in the geometry.

## Size is the top regret, so show it on the skin

Practitioner sources agree that the most common tattoo regret is size: a piece "too small to hold the detail it needs". Undersized work is described as the most regretted ([Art n Soul](https://artnsoul.net.au/how-to-choose-tattoo-size-and-placement/)). Fine lines and small lettering spread as they heal, and studios publicly decline fine-line work below a size threshold ([Visions Tattoo Gallery](https://www.visionstattoogallery.com/blog/the-fine-linethe-fineline-tattoo-craze-why-visions-tattoo-artists-wont-do-them-too-small)). Clients underestimate how much skin a design needs, and artists routinely suggest going bigger ([Sortra](https://sortra.com/why-tattoo-artists-suggest-bigger-size/)). One AR user put it plainly: a design "looked way too big on my frame ... scaled it down, got it done, love it" ([Inkjin](https://inkjin.com/en/try-on-tattoos-ar)). In retail AR research, NN/g found that scale errors are the ones users do not forgive, because "gauging the size of an item is a top reason for using an AR tool". It also found that measurements belong **in the main view**. Best Buy users who tapped a ruler icon missed the numbers because they appeared away from where they were looking ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/augmented-reality-ecommerce-guidelines/)).

Today the app shows size only in the side panel. The Width and Height sliders read in inches, and a formatted summary line sits under them (`src/ui/Panel.tsx`). The on-body box in `src/ui/DesignBox.tsx` already projects the design's centre and handles to the screen every frame. It can therefore carry a **live pill label at the design's edge** ("9.5 × 6.2 cm · 3.7 × 2.4 in") that updates during the drag, the way Figma shows dimensions as a tooltip while a handle moves ([freeCodeCamp on Figma](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-resize-elements-in-figma/)). After she lets go, the label shrinks to a small chip. This is the cheapest high-impact change in this report. The same label can open a **type-an-exact-size** field with the aspect ratio locked, and dragging can **snap to common quote sizes** (2, 3, 4, 6 in or 5, 7.5, 10, 15 cm), the way Procreate's Snapping and Magnetics lock a transform ([Procreate Handbook](https://help.procreate.com/procreate/handbook/transform/snapping)). An optional "align to limb axis" lock would make rotation as easy as sizing. A cm/in toggle that is remembered per device completes the set.

Two further cues make the number mean something to the client. The first is a **familiar reference object**. Vision research shows familiar size changes how big things look ([PMC8479574](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8479574/)), and a 2024 consumer study found photos with a size referent were rated more helpful ([Dang et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cb.2281)). A toggleable credit-card outline (ISO ID-1, 85.60 × 53.98 mm) placed on the skin next to the design is cheap to add, and so is a body-relative readout such as "covers ~60% of forearm length". The app already computes the limb's circumference for its 72% wrap rule (`src/placement/resolve.ts`), so that readout reuses an existing number. The second cue is **true scale off the screen**: a "print at 100%" PDF with a 5 cm / 2 in check bar, and, later, a one-step credit-card screen calibration for an "actual size" view ([keyboardtester ruler](https://keyboardtester.click/online-ruler.php)). On the measurement itself, for a design wrapped on a curve, the number that matches a stencil and a tape measure is the length **along the skin**, not the straight line between the edges. Because the surface wrap preserves distances on the skin, the app's width already is that number. It should say so ("along skin") so she can trust it against the printed stencil. Finally, an **S / M / L compare** of the same design on the same spot, with a soft legibility warning, turns "go bigger" into something the client can see rather than an argument. Rules of thumb put script at about **3–4 mm cap height or more**, with the thinnest strokes healing to at least about **0.3–0.4 mm** ([aifortattoo](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/script-tattoo-fonts-minimum-size-line-weight-legibility-guide)). The thresholds should be hers to edit, because the sources disagree.

## Belly, bust and thigh controls are already in Anny

Artists say the body features that change a design are soft, moving tissue and curvature. Under-bust and side-rib pieces have to "follow more pronounced contours" on fuller busts and are placed standing and braless so the line is "not tucked under the fold" ([Tattoos Wizard](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/rib-and-sternum-tattoo-guide); [Serving Some Lines](https://servingsomelines.com/blogs/tattoo-guides/underbust-tattoo-ideas-sternum-placement-guide)). Stomach pieces can warp with weight change or pregnancy ([Platinum Ink](https://platinumink.net/stomach-tattoos-after-pregnancy-what-changes/)). On the thigh, the quadriceps flattens when seated, which "severely distorts rigid geometric stencils", and on the inner thigh skin rubs on skin while healing ([Vibetat](https://vibetat.com/blog/thigh-tattoo-complete-guide-2026); [Sortra](https://sortra.com/tattoo-pain-levels-for-inner-thigh/)). Face shape barely matters. These are the controls she asked for, and the evidence supports her priorities.

Anny already contains them, which makes this cheaper than it looks. Its ~250 local changes come straight from MakeHuman/MPFB2 and include `stomach-pregnant-incr`, `breast-volume-vert-up`, `breast-trans-up`, `breast-dist-incr`, `measure-bust-circ-incr`, `hip-scale-horiz-incr`, `buttocks-volume-incr`, `l/r-upperleg-fat-incr`, `measure-thigh-circ-incr`, `l/r-leg-valgus-incr` and `l/r-upperarm-fat-incr`. All are CC0 assets under Apache-2.0 code ([naver/anny](https://github.com/naver/anny)). Local changes are **linear and additive and do not depend on the phenotype** ([phenotype.py](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/src/anny/models/phenotype.py)). Each slider can therefore be baked as one or two extra glTF morph targets on top of the existing 18-corner height × weight × muscle grid in `tools/export_bodies.py`, and still blend exactly. The Phase 1 report verified that grid to < 10⁻⁹ mm against Anny ([Phase 1 report](https://github.com/samike93/Tattoo)). One caveat matters for the hard-won elbow fix. The local targets must be passed through the same elbow-straightening skinning step as the corners, or they will not line up. The Phase 1 report notes that 18 targets grew each body from 0.84 MB to 3.8 MB, about 0.16 MB per target. A dozen more targets would add roughly 2 MB per body before compression. That cost is acceptable, but it should be budgeted.

Two of her controls need extra care. **Breast size**: Anny's global `cupsize` and `firmness` phenotypes are switched off by default (`phenotypes="all"` turns them on). They also **multiply** with gender, age, weight and muscle, so they would need extra corner bakes rather than a single target ([model_data.py](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/src/anny/models/model_data.py)). The practical route is to start with the linear local targets (`measure-bust-circ-incr` and `breast-volume-vert-up` for size, `breast-trans-up` for lift). That gives exact, cheap control now. Measure its error against cupsize with the existing `npm run measure` harness before deciding whether cupsize is worth the extra bakes. **Thigh gap**: there is **no dedicated thigh-gap target** in Anny or MPFB2. A "thigh spacing" slider has to combine `upperleg-fat`, `upperleg-scale-horiz`, `leg-valgus` and `hip-scale-horiz`. At high thigh volume the inner thighs will pass through each other, because the model has no collision. So the slider needs limits, and a design placed on the inner thigh should be checked for intersection. Height needs only a polish: add a cm entry next to feet/inches. The bisection solve already makes a short client genuinely short instead of thin-armed.

Body controls only pay off if the tattoo follows the skin as the body changes. The app already pins placements to a mesh triangle with barycentric weights, so a design stays on the same patch of skin when the body is reshaped (`src/state.ts`). The new morphs inherit that behaviour. That enables an honest "what if" view: move the belly slider and the client watches her stomach piece stretch. The sources say real outcomes are unpredictable, so this should be framed as education, not prediction. How the controls are worded matters as much as the geometry, because the client will be watching. Anny's maintainers warn that its phenotypes "encode by design stereotypes of MakeHuman artists" ([Anny tutorial](https://github.com/naver/anny/blob/main/tutorials/shape_parameterization.py)). Inclusive-avatar guidance recommends diverse starting presets backed by continuous sliders ([EGAL](https://www.egalresources.com/creating-inclusive-avatars-guide)). In practice that means "Body size" instead of "Heavy", "Bust" and "Lift" instead of "cup" and "firmness", "Belly" with a separate optional pregnancy preview, and no weight or BMI shown on screen by default. A measurement-driven body (enter bust, waist, hip and thigh circumferences) is feasible later using Anny's `anny_inverter.py` and `measure-*-circ` targets. It would need new circumference functions, and it is an offline project, not a quick win.

## Skin texture should break up the ink

For skin, what helps the conversation is how texture changes the tattoo's appearance, not more realism in general. Healed ink over stretch marks still leaves "the texture ... felt", and some areas need extra passes ([Biomaser](https://biomasertattoo.com/blogs/study/what-to-know-tattoo-over-stretch-marks)). Older skin is thinner, and "wrinkles, crepey skin, and hyperpigmentation will inevitably change your tattoo's appearance" ([Ink Art by Kate](https://inkartbykate.com/can-tattoos-cover-up-wrinkles-what-you-must-know/)). Fine lines soften and spread over time, which no AI tool shows in advance ([aifortattoo](https://www.aifortattoo.com/library/will-my-ai-tattoo-look-different-on-skin)). The app's skin shader already makes this cheap to show. Ink multiplies the skin's albedo, and the skin's own roughness and lighting sit on top (`src/ink/skinMaterial.ts`). Any wrinkle or stretch-mark normal detail added to the skin will therefore break up the ink automatically, which is how real texture behaves.

Real-time engines use a standard stack: a medium-scale normal map for wrinkles and folds, plus a small tiling micro-normal for pores, combined with angle-corrected blending ([Epic, human skin](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-human-skin-in-unreal-engine)). The app already has procedural pores, so the next layers would be the following. A **skin-age slider** raises the amplitude of a fine-wrinkle normal and lowers specular contrast. **Placeable stretch-mark and scar overlays** are thin, slightly lighter stripes with shallow normal dents, oriented horizontally on the belly, radially on bust and hips, and obliquely on thighs. Cellulite would be low-frequency dimple noise masked to the thighs and hips. Region-specific maps like these are normally painted or masked layers or procedural noise. No CC0 stretch-mark or cellulite map set was found, so they would be authored procedurally. MakeHuman's official skin packs are CC0 and share Anny's lineage ([MakeHuman license](http://www.makehumancommunity.org/content/license_explanation.html)). A separate **fresh / healed / aged ink toggle** (slight blur scaled in mm, line dilation, desaturation) addresses the fine-line regret directly. The often-quoted "lines spread 10–20% in the first year" figure is unsourced blog content ([Sortra](https://sortra.com/can-small-tattoos-blur-over-time/)), so the toggle must be labelled approximate. These features cost more than the sizing work, and their value depends on presenting them as "show my skin" rather than "flaws". That is why they rank in the middle of the roadmap, not the top.

## A consultation that feels like hers

A solo artist's edge is the relationship, and the tool should feel like her studio, not like software. Clients "should never feel like they're being difficult by asking for changes" ([Big Time Tattoos](https://www.bigtimetattoos.com/post/making-the-most-of-tattoo-consultations)). That argues for making alternatives free to try: a **Versions strip (A/B/C)** where each tweak is one tap to keep or compare, and undo for every gesture, not just delete. A **client view** for handing the iPad over would show large type, orbit, the S/M/L compare and a "favorite" heart, with pricing and tools hidden. Use her muscle memory: two-finger tap to undo matches Procreate, which she already uses. Make every handle at least 44 × 44 pt ([Apple HIG](https://developers.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inputs/touchscreen-gestures)), and add a "lock body" toggle so a stray touch does not spin the camera during fine placement. **Private client profiles** stored on her device (body settings, skin tone, notes, references, saved mockups) let a returning client open on "her" body. The app's existing promise that "designs never leave the device" becomes a selling point for intimate placements such as under-bust, hip or inner thigh. Share links should leave out body data unless she includes it. The current URL-holds-state design already leaves uploaded images out, which is the right default.

The final step closes the loop to the quote. A **consult card** generated from the mockup would carry the along-skin W × H in both units, the anatomical zone and side (needed because zones such as hands, neck, elbows and knees carry higher minimums), style, color vs black-and-grey, and flags for cover-up, scars, stretch marks or nearby tattoos. She enters estimated hours or sessions and her rate. The card applies her shop minimum and deposit and exports as a branded image or PDF with front and three-quarter views, sized for an Instagram DM. That fits where her pipeline actually runs: DMs, Porter, Venue or Tattoodo threads, and email. It also avoids the market's most-hated pattern by keeping export free.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go. The hard problem in this space, accurate scale on a curved body, is one the app has already solved and measured. Competitors and the review complaints show that almost nobody else has. What is missing is how the app presents that accuracy. The number has to live on the skin, move with her fingers, and leave the app attached to the mockup and the quote. Until it does, the app's real advantage is hidden in a side panel. The body-shape work she asked for is also lower-risk than it sounds, because Anny's belly, thigh, hip and bust controls are linear CC0 targets that slot into the existing exact-blend pipeline. Only breast phenotypes, thigh gap and skin realism need real engineering.

The larger implication is about position. Tools in this market fail on trust: sticker overlays, paywalled exports, AI detail no needle can reproduce. A tool that is honest about size, honest that body-change and healed previews are approximate, and private by default fits the way a solo artist already works with her clients. Two open questions remain, and only her consultations can answer them. No source measured whether 3D previews reduce tattoo regret, and none tested how clients react to body sliders in the chair. A few weeks of watching which controls she reaches for, and which ones make clients uncomfortable, is the next research step worth funding.
