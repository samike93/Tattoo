# Render honest ink, then capture real clients

The next gains for this app come from making the ink tell the truth and from putting it on the client's real body. A photoreal stock human would add much less. **No photoreal scanned body or skin on the market can legally ship as a downloadable GLB on a public Shopify page under the app's MIT/Apache/BSD/ISC/CC0 policy, except MakeHuman's own CC0 skins.** Those skins already fit Anny's UVs, but their quality is unverified. MetaHuman is free under $1M revenue, but it means a proprietary EULA and throwing away Anny's morph and placement system. Renderpeople and 3D Scan Store forbid exactly this kind of distribution. On the rendering side, four cheap forward-pass shader changes fit an iPad budget: a pre-integrated skin LUT, thickness transmission, a clearcoat "oily" second specular lobe, and a tiling micro-normal, plus a bundled real HDRI. Multi-pass effects (screen-space SSS, SSAO) belong only in a still "hero render". The biggest differentiators artists would notice are in the ink. Per-colour fading (azo reds and yellows go first, black lasts), a "silver skin" healing stage, region-dependent aging (hands and feet age fastest), and an objective "this colour won't show on this skin" check are all grounded in measured or consensus data. No competitor offers any of them except as unverifiable AI claims. For capture, Safari on iPad still exposes no WebXR or LiDAR depth, so the web app cannot scan anyone itself. The workable routes are importing a GLB from a free LiDAR app (about 1–1.5 cm circumference error, roughly a minute per limb) and making photo mode smarter with Apache-licensed MediaPipe. Live web AR cannot deliver true size and should not be built. Most of this evidence comes from search-engine snippets because a network proxy blocked primary pages. The table below says which claims were checked against full text.

## Only CC0 MakeHuman skins clear both the licence bar and the UV bar

A three.js page on a Shopify storefront serves its GLB and textures as plain network requests that any visitor can save. That one fact eliminates almost every photoreal source. **Renderpeople** allows real-time and game use, but its terms say customers may not make the data available "in such a way that the 3D data can be downloaded, extracted, distributed or accessed by third parties as single files" ([Renderpeople T&C §4](https://renderpeople.com/general-terms-and-conditions/), snippet). Its range is clothed in any case. **3D Scan Store (Ten24)** bans "public sharing of the scan or any derivative in any 3D format" and "integration of scan data into character maker programmes where results can be exported". The second clause could catch a body-shape editor with image export. Its commercial project licence runs about **+£1,943** even for a head pack ([3D Scan Store licensing](https://www.3dscanstore.com/terms-and-conditions-licensing); [10× HD Head Pack](https://www.3dscanstore.com/hd-head-scans/hd-head-bundles/10xhd-head-scan-pack-05), snippets). **HumanScanRepository** prohibits redistribution "outside the agreed commercial project" ([Licensing Guide](https://humanscanrepository.com/licensing-guide/), snippet). **TexturingXYZ** micro-detail maps may be used only "incorporated into permitted end products" ([TexturingXYZ terms](https://texturing.xyz/pages/terms-of-service), snippet). JavaScript obfuscation does not change this: the browser must decode the data to draw it. None of these vendors is usable without a negotiated, written web licence.

**MetaHuman** is the tempting exception, and the answer is still no for this app. Since the June 2025 release with UE 5.6, Epic says MetaHumans "can be used with any engine or creative software". It treats exported characters as royalty-free "Non-Engine Products", free under **$1M** annual revenue and requiring about **$1,850 per seat per year** above that ([metahuman.com/license](https://www.metahuman.com/license); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/), snippets). The "asset files … including in products that use or rely on other video game engines" wording was read verbatim, but only in the **2021** EULA PDF ([UE EULA for Creators 2021](https://s3.amazonaws.com/EULA/04be6996-1cf1-4f50-9e75-b22db4680516-EULA.pdf)). The live license page still returns older "UE-Only Content" wording that contradicts the new position. That conflict is unresolved. Any Fab add-on, such as a nude skin texture, carries the Fab rule that "you must restrict end users from extracting" the content ([Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula?lang=en), snippet). A public GLB breaches that. Three further problems are practical rather than legal:

- The default body has **underwear baked into the texture and no genitals** ([Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/metahumans-underwear/238833), snippet).
- There is no glTF export; the path is UE → FBX → Blender → GLB.
- MetaHuman topology shares nothing with Anny, so adopting it would throw away the exact-blend morph grid, the triangle-pinned placements and the distortion measurements the app is built on.

The UE EULA is also proprietary, so it would break the stated licence policy.

The permissive options are thin. **Microsoft Rocketbox** is genuinely MIT, verified in its README, with 115 rigged avatars ([Rocketbox README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox/master/README.md)). But its clothing is "hard modeled into their bodies" ([PSU review](https://sites.psu.edu/alexfatemi/2022/08/11/rocketbox_review/), snippet), at about 10k triangles with 2048² textures. The skin a tattoo needs is under fabric. **HSRD-100** from Digital Reality Lab is the only high-end real scan set that can legally ship: CC BY 4.0, 100 scans, 16k/8k/4k textures ([Digital Reality Lab](https://www.digitalrealitylab.com/blog/hsrd-100/), snippet). It is clothed, posed, on its own topology, and needs attribution, which falls outside the CC0-only policy. Hobby CC-BY scans on Sketchfab and Polycam's CC BY 4.0 Explore captures ([Polycam usage rights](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/27646853145748-Usage-Rights-and-Ownership), snippet) add a risk the licence cannot cover. CC licenses the uploader's copyright, not the scanned person's likeness. **SMPL/SMPL-X, THuman2.0 and AGORA are non-commercial** and stay excluded, as the project's licence log already records ([SMPL licence](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [THuman2.0](https://github.com/ytrock/THuman2.0-Dataset)).

That leaves **MakeHuman/MPFB2**. Its licence (read verbatim) puts "Textures" among the assets "released under CC0 1.0 Universal… anyone can do whatever they want with it" ([MPFB2 LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/master/LICENSE.md)). The community packs **skins01** (natural female, about 99 MB) and **skins02** (natural male, about 72 MB) are listed as CC0 ([skins01](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/skins01.html); [skins02](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/skins02.html), snippets). These are painted for the hm08 UV layout that Anny inherits, so they should map without re-baking. What is unknown is whether they are any good. The system skin files run about 3.5–4.1 MB per PNG ([tuxfamily mirror](https://download.tuxfamily.org/makehuman/assets/1.1/base/skins/textures/), snippet), which suggests roughly 2k diffuse-only maps. MPFB's own "enhanced skin" adds procedural bump on top ([MPFB docs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/docs/materials/skinv1.html), snippet), which suggests the same. The "hairless" and "uniform" entries are the ones likely to be free of baked hair or underwear. Community uploaders asserted CC0 for their own paintings, so keep a dated record of each pack page. The one CC0 detail source found is "Human Materials 1 (CC0)", a set of seamless 2k tiling skin maps with colour, AO, roughness, normal and displacement ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/human-materials-1-cc0-be48a7526b304914b7c9f45c289b2ecf), snippet). Its author says they made it because public-domain skin textures barely exist. This matters more than it looks. A photographic albedo would also collide with the app's melanin-driven ITA skin-tone model, because a photo bakes in one person's tone. A tiling micro-detail layer over the existing procedural colour keeps every skin tone available.

## Four forward-pass shader changes buy most of the remaining skin realism on iPad

The app's skin is MeshPhysicalMaterial patched through `onBeforeCompile`. It has per-channel wrap lighting, procedural pores, sheen, regional colour, per-vertex AO and a procedural studio environment (`src/ink/skinMaterial.ts`, `src/ui/Scene.tsx`). The research supports four upgrades that cost almost nothing per pixel. The first is a **pre-integrated (Penner) skin LUT** to replace the wrap term. It is a single-pass method that bakes the diffusion profile into a texture indexed by N·L and curvature, so its runtime cost is "only a LUT lookup" ([Penner & Borshukov, GPU Pro 2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Pre-Integrated-Skin-Shading-Penner-Borshukov/9e7f19fba55066e98f9d8c8fc4511071c548ad20); [Appocrypha](https://suhyeokkim.github.io/2022/04/30/two-sss-skin-represation-method-for-game)). Unreal lists it on its mobile roadmap ([UE roadmap](https://portal.productboard.com/epicgames/1-unreal-engine-public-roadmap/c/453-mobile-rendering-improvements)), and a reference implementation includes a curve fit that avoids the texture fetch ([codewings/PreIntegrated-Skin](https://github.com/codewings/PreIntegrated-Skin), licence unverified). Curvature can be baked per vertex in `tools/export_bodies.py` next to AO and regions. The second is **thickness transmission** for ears, fingers, nostrils and finger webbing. three.js already ships the DICE term under MIT (default distortion 0.1, power 2.0, scale 10.0), verified from source ([three.js SubsurfaceScatteringShader.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/examples/jsm/shaders/SubsurfaceScatteringShader.js)), and there is a known pattern for patching it into the physical material ([mattdesl gist](https://gist.github.com/mattdesl/2ee82157a86962347dedb6572142df7c)). Avoid `MeshPhysicalMaterial.transmission`. It is refraction through an extra scene pass, not skin scattering (unverified recollection, flagged in the notes).

The third change is **specular breakup**, which is where skin realism actually lives. MetaHuman's skin uses two specular lobes whose combination "provide[s] nice subpixel micro-frequencies" ([Epic Digital Humans](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/en-US/Resources/Showcases/DigitalHumans), snippet). Epic layers a tiling micro-normal over a wrinkle-only normal map ([Epic, human skin](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/creating-human-skin-in-unreal-engine), snippet), and open shaders in the Activision lineage combine dual lobes with cavity occlusion ([Safemilk/SkinShader](https://github.com/Safemilk/SkinShader)). In three.js the cheapest second lobe is the existing **clearcoat**. As an inferred starting point, try clearcoat about 0.05–0.15 at roughness 0.25–0.35 over base roughness 0.5–0.6, masked regionally: stronger on the T-zone and lips, and over fresh ink. Swap the regular procedural pores for a tiling CC0 micro-normal blended with reoriented normal mapping. Fade it by `fwidth`, and widen roughness from normal variance so pores don't sparkle on a Retina screen. The fourth is a **real HDRI**. The app chose procedural lighting because the Poly Haven download was blocked and a runtime fetch would break offline use (`docs/decisions.md`). Poly Haven assets are CC0 ([Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/)), so a 1k studio HDRI bundled into the build keeps the app offline. PMREM prefiltering is a one-time cost, and dual-lobe specular only looks right when it reflects a believable environment.

Everything multi-pass should be reserved for a still image. Jimenez's separable SSS is the quality benchmark, with "under 0.5 milliseconds" and seven samples per pixel, but those are PC GPU figures from around 2015 ([TU Wien](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2015/Jimenez_SSS_2015/)). On the web it needs a diffuse-only target and two full-screen blur passes. Its BSD-2 reference is DirectX/HLSL and must carry an attribution line ([separable-sss README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iryoku/separable-sss/master/README.md)). **N8AO** is CC0, with a "Performance" preset of 8+4 samples meant for mobile and a half-resolution mode giving a "2x-4x" speed-up ([N8AO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/N8python/n8ao/master/README.md)). The app's earlier decision to reject full-screen AO on iPad still stands for the live view. No measured millisecond figures exist for any of these on any Apple GPU in WebGL or WebGPU, and every iPad cost estimate in the notes is a guess. Mobile guidance consistently says to cap the device pixel ratio at about 2, which "can double mobile frame rate" ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)), and to drop bloom and SSAO first. Since the scene is mostly static, the better split is to **render on demand for the live view, and when the artist exports, render once at high quality** (supersampled, separable SSS, N8AO "Medium"). That export is the image that ends up in the Instagram DM and the quote. Safari 26 turned WebGPU on by default across Apple platforms ([WebKit blog](https://webkit.org/blog/16993/news-from-wwdc25-web-technology-coming-this-fall-in-safari-26-beta/)). A migration would mean rewriting every `onBeforeCompile` patch in TSL, though, so WebGL2 should stay primary until a feature demands otherwise. Hair is low priority for a tattoo tool. If scalp hair or lashes are ever wanted, an MIT three.js hair-card shader covers both WebGL and WebGPU ([threejs-hair-shader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/creategamecharacters/threejs-hair-shader/main/README.md)).

## Real ink is a filtered, blurred, slowly fading layer, and the shader is most of the way there

The physics is settled even where the measurements are missing. Tattoo pigment sits in the dermis under a melanin-bearing epidermis, inside macrophages that die, release the pigment and get replaced. That cycle keeps the ink roughly in place while it migrates slowly deeper and sideways ([Baranska et al. 2018, J Exp Med](https://rupress.org/jem/article/215/4/1115/42419/Unveiling-skin-macrophage-dynamics-explains-both); [Grant et al. 2015](https://www.beilstein-journals.org/bjnano/articles/6/120), abstracts). In vivo photothermal radiometry puts the effective optical depth of the ink layer at about **300 µm** ([Milanič/Majaron, SPIE 2012](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012SPIE.8207E..0GM/abstract)). Histology and reviews quote 0.6–2.2 mm for professional work. These measure different things (optical centroid versus total extent), not a contradiction. Deep black reads blue-grey because short wavelengths back-scatter in the dermis above it. Dermatology calls this the Tyndall effect ([PMC: traumatic tattoos](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11626363/)), and diffuse-reflectance work notes that aging black "will shift to a blue tone" ([Rudraiah 2022 via d-nb.info](https://d-nb.info/1251592023/34)). The app's shader already gets the structure right. Ink multiplies the skin albedo, and pores, roughness and sheen stay on top. Healed and aged ink spread by a real-millimetre radius, and aged blacks lift toward blue-grey. The UI is honest that these are estimates. What follows is the gap between that model and the evidence.

**Skin tone: the multiply already encodes the melanin filter.** Melanin absorption follows Jacques's power law, μa = 6.6×10¹¹·λ^−3.33 cm⁻¹ ([OMLC skin optics](https://omlc.org/news/jan98/skinoptics.html), snippet). It is strongest at the blue end, so light passing down to the ink and back up loses blue far more than red. The researchers' worked example (an inference with unverified melanin fractions) puts round-trip transmission at 450 nm near **0.79 for very light skin and 0.03 for very dark skin**. Because the app's skin albedo already comes from measured-style L\*a\*b\* values per ITA category, multiplying ink into it reproduces this filter without per-tone rules. Practitioners report the result: black and saturated deep reds, blues, greens and purples stay readable on deep skin, while pastels, yellows and white fade from view ([Starbrite Colors](https://www.starbritecolors.com/blogs/articles/best-tattoo-ink-colors-for-dark-skin-tones); [Tommy's Supplies](https://www.tommyssupplies.com/blogs/news/what-you-need-to-know-about-tattoo-inks-for-darker-skin), practitioner). The multiply model has two gaps. It has no **epidermal veil**: real black ink never reaches the near-zero albedo a pure multiply gives, because the epidermis scatters some skin-coloured light back before it reaches the ink. And it cannot render **white or light ink** at all, since multiplying by white changes nothing. The first gap needs a small veil term that grows with melanin and with the healing state. The second needs a melanin-attenuated lightening term for light inks. Both are inferences without measured constants. No peer-reviewed L\*a\*b\* table of the same ink across Fitzpatrick I–VI turned up. Do not estimate tone automatically in photo mode either. A 2026 paper finds ITA from uncontrolled phone photos unreliable ([Alipour 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/srt.70374), abstract). Let the artist pick the tone, or calibrate it against a grey card.

That physics supports an objective **ink visibility check**. Compute ΔE2000 between each colour in the design, rendered on the client's skin, and the bare skin around it. Flag colours below a threshold the artist sets, with wording like "this yellow may barely show on this skin". Artists already give this advice verbally. The tool would turn it into something the client can see. The practitioner claim that blues and greens "pop" on dark skin is not explained by transmission alone. It is probably hue contrast against warm, orange-brown skin, which is another reason to use a hue-aware ΔE metric rather than a lightness rule.

**Healing has a stage the app skips.** The measured picture is large early pigment loss. In mice, Pigment Red 22 dropped about **32% by day 42** ([Engel et al. 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26763404_Tattooing_of_skin_results_in_transportation_and_light-induced_decomposition_of_tattoo_pigments-a_first_quantification_in_vivo_using_a_mouse_model), abstract). In old human tattoos, red azo pigment was **87–99% lower** than in fresh ones ([Lehner et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21349116)). Visible change is far smaller because optical density saturates. The one colorimetric time series found, on nipple-areola tattoos with a colour chart, showed significant change between every timepoint to 12 months, ending **9% brighter** than the untreated side ([PMC11288766](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11288766/), abstract). Re-epithelialisation takes 2–3 weeks and epidermal maturation 8–12 ([An Bras Dermatol 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0365059624000345)). Between them sits the stage clients panic about: the milky "silver skin" phase. Practitioners say it appears around weeks 2–4, clears over about 2–4 months, and makes colours look most muted and lines softest ([xpaintattoo](https://xpaintattoo.com/blogs/news/the-milky-phase-why-your-healing-tattoo-looks-cloudy-or-faded), practitioner). A fourth ink look, **"Healing (wk 2–6)"**, would be a whitish semi-opaque veil with extra blur. It would be cheap to add and useful as aftercare education. "Healed" should read about 5–15% lighter than fresh, anchored on that single 9% figure.

**Aging is colour- and place-specific; the shader treats it as uniform.** Today `uInkFade` desaturates every colour equally. The evidence says otherwise. Azo reds and yellows are the photolabile ones: PR 22 was almost completely destroyed by **110 days of natural sunlight** in solution ([Engel et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17610608/)). Black is the most stable, and phthalocyanine blues and greens are generally regarded as stable, though that was not confirmed this session. A per-hue fade multiplier (yellow, orange and red highest; pastels and white next; blue and green lower; black lowest) is a small shader change and a real consultation talking point. Location matters as much. The only expert-weighted placement scale is the laser-removal Kirby-Desai scale: head and neck 1, upper trunk 2, lower trunk 3, proximal extremity 4, distal extremity 5, reflecting blood and lymphatic supply ([Kirby et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2923953/)). Practitioners add that hands, fingers, feet and lips fade and blow out fastest ([OSCO London](https://www.oscolondon.studio/tattoo-aftercare-1/do-hand-tattoos-fade), practitioner). The app already knows each placement's anatomical region, so an aging multiplier per region (hands, fingers and feet 2–3×, sun-exposed distal limbs moderate, trunk baseline) is cheap. It must be labelled illustrative, since Kirby-Desai measures removal response, not natural fading. Real spread also differs by channel: the skin's diffusion profile spreads red further than blue ([GPU Gems 3 ch.14](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-14-advanced-techniques-realistic-real-time-skin), snippet). Low-amplitude density noise along strokes would mimic needle-depth variation and break the vector-perfect look.

**Line spread has no peer-reviewed rate, so the app should lint designs rather than predict them.** No longitudinal study measures tattoo line width over years. The widely repeated "lines expand 10–20% in the first year" has no primary source ([aifortattoo](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/script-tattoo-fonts-minimum-size-line-weight-legibility-guide), practitioner). Practitioner rules cluster tightly enough to act on, though: no parallel fine lines closer than about **2 mm**, gaps at least **1.5× the stroke width**, block lettering at least about **6 mm** and script about **12–13 mm** tall ([tattooswizard](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/fine-line-tattoos-how-they-age); [sortra](https://sortra.com/how-big-should-a-tattoo-be-for-lettering/), practitioner). The app knows the design's true printed size in millimetres, so it can measure stroke widths and gaps in the rasterised design and warn at the chosen size. The thresholds should be the artist's to edit, because sources range from 2 mm to 1/8 in (3.2 mm).

**Placement distortion is a pose problem.** Stomach, hips, inner arm, chest, ribs and thighs change most with weight and pregnancy, and joints and torso distort with movement. A stencil placed while the client stands warps when they sit or twist, and geometric and mandala designs show it most ([sortra](https://sortra.com/how-tattoo-style-affects-placement/); [Skin Illustrations](https://skinillustrations.net/blog/tattoo-placement-guide-pros-cons-and-pain-levels/), practitioner). No source quantifies distortion per kilogram or per degree of flexion. The app's body sliders already let a client watch a belly piece stretch. The next step is a cheap per-region "distortion-prone" flag on the consult output. A seated or arm-bent pose for flagged regions is the expensive follow-on. Blowout is a technique failure, not aging, and should never appear in the "aged" preview.

## The client's own body beats any stock model, but Safari cannot scan it

The licensing dead end in the first section points to a different source of realism: the client. Free native LiDAR apps already produce scaled meshes. **Scaniverse** is free and exports OBJ, FBX, GLB and USDZ with on-device processing ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/scaniverse-3d-scanner/id1541433223), snippet). **KIRI Engine 4.0** (September 2025) removed the free tier's export cap and exports GLB/glTF/USDZ among others ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/09/kiri-engine-4-0-is-out-for-android-and-ios/), snippet). **3d Scanner App** exports GLB from LiDAR or TrueDepth ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/3d-scanner-app/id1419913995), snippet). Polycam's free tier is glTF-only with public share links, which is a privacy problem for semi-nude scans ([SkyeBrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/polycam-review), snippet). Accuracy on people is good enough for a preview and not for final sizing:

- Sub-1.5% error on body lengths with an iPad Pro ([Sensors 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24020500), abstract).
- Polycam circumferences consistently **7.8–15.0 mm smaller** than a depth-sensor reference, with ICC above 0.80, while a rigid mannequin came within 3.5 mm. The authors blame subject motion, not reconstruction ([arXiv 2608.12827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12827), snippet).
- A LiDAR limb scan takes **64 ± 15 s**. The thigh and foot are the weakest sites ([Biosensors 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/bios15060381), abstract).
- About half of athletes' lower-limb scans were incomplete or distorted ([Sports 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports12040092), abstract).

A 1–1.5 cm error on a 30 cm forearm is roughly 3–5% in surface size. That is fine for placement, and a tape check should still confirm the final size. The practical protocol follows from motion being the main error: client seated, limb braced on the bench, one limb per scan.

The web app cannot do the scanning itself. Safari on iOS and iPadOS implements no WebXR immersive-ar, the feature flag is "not in a testable state", and the Depth Sensing module has no Safari support ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/756850); [W3C Depth Sensing](https://immersive-web.github.io/depth-sensing/)). Because WebKit is mandatory on iOS, no other browser can add it ([XRDoctors 2026](https://xrdoctors.pro/blog/webxr-on-ios-what-actually-works)). Apple's Object Capture is a native Swift API ([WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10076/?time=416)). That leaves three routes. The first is **import**: AirDrop or Files a GLB from a scanning app into the web app. This costs the least and has the highest accuracy. The second is a **native companion app or App Clip** that uploads a scan, the best experience at the cost of App Store work. The third is **browser photogrammetry** from camera frames, which has no metric scale and suffers worse from motion. Imported scans will be noisy, non-manifold and carry baked lighting. The app's surface-wrap placement was built on Anny's welded mesh, so a scan will need decimation and welding before placement can run on it. Import has one benefit no stock body can match: **the scan's texture shows the client's existing tattoos, scars and stretch marks.** Those are exactly what an artist needs when fitting a piece between old work or planning a cover-up. This is the researchers' inference, but it lines up with how artists already use client photos.

Photo-only body estimation is mostly a licensing minefield. NAVER's **Multi-HMR 2**, which regresses Anny meshes from images, is under a licence (read verbatim) that says "You may not use the Materials or derivatives thereof for any commercial purpose" ([multi-hmr2 LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/naver/multi-hmr2/main/LICENSE.txt)). Meta **Sapiens v1 is CC-BY-NC** ([HF facebook/sapiens](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sapiens)). **Sapiens2** (April 2026) has a custom licence that bars "biometric processing". It was read only through an LLM summary, so it needs legal review before use ([Sapiens2 LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sapiens2/main/LICENSE.md)). The commercially clean stack is **MediaPipe BlazePose GHUM** (Apache 2.0, runs in the browser, 33 landmarks with 36–45 mm 3D joint error) plus MediaPipe segmentation ([BlazePose model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf)). Its output is landmarks, not a surface, and photo-based circumference estimates miss by several centimetres ([PMC11606355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11606355), snippet). That makes it a poor body builder and a good helper for **photo mode**. Today the artist sets the limb curve by hand and calibrates scale by tapping two ends of a ruler (`src/photo/`). Pose landmarks could set the limb axis and rotation automatically. The segmentation silhouette could give the limb's pixel width, and from that the curve radius, and could fade the ink at the limb's edge. A credit-card outline (85.60 × 53.98 mm) would be a faster scale reference than a ruler. Use the CPU delegate for long sessions: the GPU delegate leaks GL handles in continuous video segmentation and can abort without float textures ([mediapipe #6352](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/6352); [#6296](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/6296)).

**Live web AR is not worth building.** Without depth, body-landmark anchoring jitters by tens of millimetres. The category's pioneer, Inkhunter, stabilises its overlay by tracking a smiley drawn in pen on the skin and has the user pinch to size it ([Gearbrain](https://www.gearbrain.com/review-inkhunter-virtual-tattoo-parlor-2431877811.html), snippet). It does not achieve calibrated size either. The freeze-frame photo mode the app already has is the more robust form of the same idea.

Capture makes privacy a design requirement. Body scans used to identify someone are GDPR Article 9 special-category data needing explicit consent ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2018/11/law-a-la-mode-27th-edition-november-2018/123d-body-scanning); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/processing-biometric-data-be-careful-under-the-gdpr)). The app's rule that photos never leave the device should extend to scans:

- Keep scans in IndexedDB on the studio iPad.
- Never put them in share links.
- Delete them after the session by default.
- Prompt for consent at import.
- Steer artists away from cloud-processing and public-link app tiers.

## Competitors split realism, scale and honesty between them

Every competitor fact below comes from search snippets of store and vendor pages. Prices and ratings may be months old, and several conflict. The market has four camps. The first is **live AR overlays**: Inkhunter, Inkjin, TattooTune, InkStudio and Tattoodo's try-on. Inkjin claims "real 3D tracking" that wraps the arm, free in the browser ([Inkjin](https://inkjin.com/en/try-on-tattoos-ar)). Inkhunter now sells itself as "AI Tattoo Design" and draws billing complaints ([Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/unveil-inkhunter-app-feedback-analysis-essential-insights-app-store-us-150700)). Its ratings conflict between sources (about 4.7★ from 25K, or 3.6★ from 3,374). The second is **generative photo blending**: Tatship, Tatspark, InkPreview, Tat Ink, BlackInk and Fotor. Tatship is 4.7★ from 691 ratings, at $12.99 a week or $37.99 a year in one snippet ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tatship-tattoo-design-try-on/id6746807416)). InkPreview claims "real, healed ink on skin" ([InkPreview](https://www.inkpreview.app/)), and AgeMe.Tattoo claims 50-year aging ([AgeMe](https://ageme.tattoo/)). Neither explains its method. The third is **3D body viewers**. **Try Tattoo 3D** on the web costs $4.99 one-time or $4.99 a month ([pricing](https://trytattoo3d.com/pricing/)) and shows about **$210 of revenue in 30 days** on TrustMRR ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/try-tattoo-3d)). The new iPad app **"Tattoo Preview – 3D & AR"** has unknown traction ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tattoo-preview-3d-ar/id6760173684)). The fourth is the **professional habit**: Procreate at $12.99 plus purchased body OBJs. Tattoo Smart sells a 48-model collection for $49.99 in four body types ([Tattoo Smart](https://tattoosmart.com/products/model-humans-collection)), and buyers report designs that "distort at the edge" ([Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1155856741/procreate-3d-female-model-procreate), review summary). The timing favours honesty. About **one in four enquiries** at one Adelaide studio now include AI-generated images, and artists say those images cannot be reproduced on skin ([ABC News, Aug 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-06/rise-in-ai-generated-tattoo-requests/106945744); [Euronews, Sep 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/09/would-you-let-ai-design-your-next-tattoo-tattoo-artists-say-ai-is-changing-the-way-they-wo)). Generative try-ons beat this app on photorealism of a single still. They cannot guarantee the artist's exact linework or scale, and their healed and aged claims are unverifiable. The opening is a non-generative preview that shows what the needle will actually produce on this person's skin, at true size, and how it will change. No competitor found does colour-specific aging, region-specific aging, ink-visibility warnings, a healing-stage view, design legibility linting or a hand-off to quoting. The main threats are the new iPad 3D app, which is the same concept with unknown depth; Inkjin's free curvature-aware AR, if its "real scale" claim holds; and the entrenched one-time-cost Procreate habit. Market size for studio-side preview tools is unknown, and Try Tattoo 3D's revenue suggests 3D preview on its own does not sell. The value has to come from consult and quote workflow.

Some things should be deliberately left off the list. Do not adopt **MetaHuman**: it breaks licence policy, replaces Anny wholesale, and its extraction wording is unresolved. ## Conclusion

The research turns the realism question on its head. For this app, a more lifelike stock human is both the most legally constrained upgrade and the least useful one. Every high-end body is either proprietary, clothed or non-commercial, and the one licence-clean photo skin is of unknown quality. The realism that matters in a consult is in the ink, and there the app is ahead because it already treats ink as a layer under the skin and not a sticker. Pushing that model to colour-specific, place-specific and stage-specific behaviour is cheap shader work. It turns the preview into an honest forecast that artists can stand behind, in a market where generative tools are training clients to expect tattoos no needle can make. Realism of the body itself should come from the client: a smarter photo mode now, scan import next, and measurement fitting later, all kept on the device.

Three unknowns deserve real measurement before marketing copy leans on them. First, the actual frame cost of each shader change on the oldest iPad the studio uses; no published Apple-GPU numbers exist. Second, whether the MakeHuman CC0 skins look better than the procedural skin at all. Third, how far a rendered "aged" or "visibility" warning matches real healed work on real clients. The artist's own portfolio of healed photos, shot under consistent light with a colour chart as the nipple-areola study did, is the only dataset that could calibrate the ink model. Collecting it is the next research investment worth making.
