# How Tattoo Ink Actually Looks on Real Skin (skin tone, healing, aging, optics, placement)

> Method note for the report writer: WebSearch worked, but full-text fetches of nearly every academic host (PubMed/PMC, ScienceDirect, Wiley, Springer, Nature, MDPI, Beilstein, RUPress, OMLC, JCAD, d-nb.info) were blocked by the network egress proxy in this session. Peer-reviewed findings below therefore come from search-result abstracts and snippets attributed to the named papers, not from reading the full text. Exact numbers are reported only where the snippet stated them. Each finding is labelled **[MEASURED]** (peer-reviewed or instrument data), **[CLINICAL SCALE]** (expert-consensus clinical tool), or **[PRACTITIONER]** (artist, studio, ink-maker or blog opinion). Many practitioner blogs (sortra.com, aifortattoo, cangguinkclub, etc.) look SEO- or AI-generated. Treat them as signals of what artists commonly say, not as evidence.

## 1. Ink on different skin tones (Fitzpatrick / ITA)

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed L\*a\*b\* or spectrophotometry dataset of tattooed versus untattooed skin across Fitzpatrick I–VI turned up in this search. The physics is well established: epidermal melanin sits above the ink and filters light on the way in and on the way out. The practitioner consensus is consistent: black and dark, saturated "jewel" tones stay readable on deep skin, while pastels, whites, yellows and light colours fade from view. A renderer can model this directly as melanin-filtered, two-pass transmission over the ink layer and does not need ad-hoc per-tone rules.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED / standard]** Objective skin-tone axis: ITA° = arctan((L\* − 50)/b\*) × 180/π (Chardon et al. 1991). Categories: Very light > 55°; Light 41–55°; Intermediate 28–41°; Tan 10–28°; Brown −30° to 10°; Dark < −30°. ITA correlates inversely with melanin content. — [Henry Ford Health Scholarly Commons (ITA categories)](https://scholarlycommons.henryford.com/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1624&context=dermatology_articles); [Scarletred ITA explainer](https://www.scarletred.com/articles/scarletred-r-vision-a-scalable-digital-solution-for-measurement-of-the-individual-typology-angle-itadeg-and-changes-in-the-skin-tone); [JID "Cutaneous Colorimetry" Research Techniques Made Simple](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022202X19333974)
- **[MEASURED / standard]** In CIELAB skin colorimetry, L\* (0–100) tracks pigmentation, a\* tracks redness/erythema, and b\* tracks yellow/pigmentation/tan. Clinics increasingly pair Fitzpatrick with ITA° or melanin-index spectrophotometry because Fitzpatrick is subjective and based on UV response. — [JID Cutaneous Colorimetry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022202X19333974); [EyeWiki: Fitzpatrick Scale](https://eyewiki.org/The_Fitzpatrick_Scale)
- **[MEASURED, caveat]** A 2026 Skin Research & Technology paper says ITA computed from uncontrolled photos (phone/camera) is unreliable for skin-type estimation. This matters if the app estimates tone from client photos. — [Alipour 2026, "Limitations of ITA ... Uncontrolled Imaging Conditions"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/srt.70374)
- **[MEASURED, laser-removal side]** In laser removal, higher Fitzpatrick types clear more slowly. Operators use lower fluence and longer intervals to avoid pigmentary side effects, and the Kirby-Desai scale scores skin type 1–6 as a direct additive term. — [Kirby-Desai scale (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/45800359_The_Kirby-Desai_Scale_A_Proposed_Scale_to_Assess_Tattoo-removal_Treatments); [LaserAway explainer](https://www.laseraway.com/articles/tattoo-removal/laser-tattoo-removal-101-what-is-the-kirby-desai-scale/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Mechanism as artists describe it: ink sits about 1–2 mm deep in the dermis under a melanin-loaded epidermis. Light passes through the epidermis, hits the ink and returns, so deeper skin tones mute pastels and whites the most. — [Tattoo Near Me AU, 2026 skin-tone guide](https://tattoonearme.com.au/blog/tattoos-different-skin-types-tones-guide)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Type V (brown): black, deep reds, blues and purples show well, and white disappears. Type VI: bold black and grey and densely packed colour only, with design prioritised over colour. — [Tattoo Near Me AU](https://tattoonearme.com.au/blog/tattoos-different-skin-types-tones-guide)
- **[PRACTITIONER / ink manufacturer]** Melanin "acts like a filter": lighter, less saturated inks are muted or hidden on deeper skin. Black, deep red, emerald green, royal blue and purple are the colours most often recommended. — [Starbrite Colors (ink maker)](https://www.starbritecolors.com/blogs/articles/best-tattoo-ink-colors-for-dark-skin-tones); [Tommy's Supplies](https://www.tommyssupplies.com/blogs/news/what-you-need-to-know-about-tattoo-inks-for-darker-skin); [Removery](https://removery.com/blog/tattoos-on-dark-skin/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** "Black is the most consistently visible ink on any skin tone." Deep blues and blue-greens are said to contrast better than warm colours against warm-undertoned dark skin. The recommended technique is to use black for edges and values and add colour as a secondary accent. — [Starbrite: which colours show up, hold & heal](https://www.starbritecolors.com/blogs/articles/tattooing-on-dark-skin-which-ink-colors-show-up-hold-heal-best); [aifortattoo dark-skin palette (low-quality source)](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/best-tattoo-colors-for-dark-skin-palette-contrast-linework)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** "Lighter, pale tattoo ink colors simply will not be seen on darker skin because the large supply of melanin filters them out." — [Tommy's Supplies](https://www.tommyssupplies.com/blogs/news/can-people-with-dark-skin-get-colored-tattoos-here-s-what-you-need-to-know)
- **[MEASURED, proxy data]** The closest colorimetric time series found is a nipple-areola tattoo study (59 Japanese patients, 60 sites). It used standardised photos with a colour chart (Casmatch) and measured RGB and luminance before treatment, immediately after, and at 1 week and 1, 3, 6 and 12 months. RGB and luminance differed significantly between neighbouring timepoints, green changed at every interval, fading slowed over time but continued between 6 and 12 months, and at 12 months the tattooed area was 9% brighter (luminance) than the untreated contralateral NAC. Dark and reddish pigments tended to reproduce colour better. — [Fading and Color Reproducibility of NAC Tattoos in Asian Patients, 2024 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11288766/); [PubMed 39086372](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39086372)
- **[MEASURED, model]** An older biomedical-engineering paper predicted the colour of tattooed skin with Monte Carlo light transport using measured optical-coefficient spectra of skin and tattoo dyes, and validated it by comparing calculated and measured reflectance as L\*a\*b\* distances. — ["Experimental and numerical study of the colour appearance of tattoo models", Med Biol Eng Comput (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02348128)

### Inferences
- **Rendering model (physics-consistent):** treat the tattoo as a pigment layer under the epidermis. Displayed ink colour ≈ skin-diffuse colour × ink reflectance, attenuated by epidermal melanin transmission applied twice, once on the way in and once on the way out. With Jacques's melanosome absorption μa,mel(λ) = 6.6×10¹¹ · λ^−3.33 cm⁻¹ (λ in nm; see section 6) and an epidermis about 60 µm thick, T(λ) = exp(−2 · f_mel · μa,mel(λ) · 0.006 cm).
  - μa,mel works out to about 970 cm⁻¹ at 450 nm, 500 cm⁻¹ at 550 nm and 280 cm⁻¹ at 650 nm.
  - At an illustrative f_mel = 0.02 (light skin), T is about 0.79 at 450 nm and 0.89 at 550 nm.
  - At f_mel = 0.30 (very dark skin), T is about 0.03 at 450 nm and 0.17 at 550 nm.
  - This f_mel → T worked example is my own calculation from the cited formula. The f_mel values are illustrative and not verified in this session (see Gaps).
- The steep blue-end attenuation explains why pale and cool light colours vanish on dark skin, and why yellow and white lose contrast: the skin around them is already dark in the same wavelengths. On dark skin, what clients see is **contrast against the surrounding skin**, not absolute ink colour. Light inks become close to the skin colour, and black stays darker than the skin but with smaller luminance contrast than on light skin. The practitioner claim that deep blues and greens "pop" on dark skin is not explained by transmission alone. It is likely a hue contrast against warm, orange-brown skin, which supports a hue-aware contrast metric (e.g. ΔE2000 between rendered tattoo and skin) for client warnings.
- For client guidance, a computed ΔE between each ink and the client's skin would let the app flag "this colour may not be visible on your skin" objectively instead of by Fitzpatrick label alone.
- The NAC study's 9% luminance gain at 12 months gives one usable number: plan for healed and settled pigment to read roughly 5–10% lighter than fresh (one study, one population, cosmetic pigment).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed spectrophotometric study of the same ink colours tattooed across Fitzpatrick I–VI turned up, and no published L\*a\*b\* table of "black on type II versus black on type VI". The full texts that might hold this (Springer tattoo-colour paper, JID colorimetry review) could not be fetched.
- Melanin volume fractions by skin type (often quoted from the OMLC/Jacques skin-optics summary as about 1–6% light, about 11–16% moderate, about 18–43% dark) could not be verified because omlc.org was blocked. Treat them as unverified until checked.
- White ink on dark skin (yellowing, keloid/raised risk) and hypo- or hyperpigmentation after tattooing deeper skin were not quantified in any source found.

## 2. Healing timeline: fresh versus healed (4–6 weeks) versus settled

### Takeaway
Fresh tattoos look darker, more saturated and crisp, with redness and slight swelling. Peeling follows in weeks 1–2, then a cloudy, milky "silver skin" phase around weeks 2–4, which artists say clears over about 2–4 months. Measured data show large early pigment loss: about 32% of a red pigment lost by 42 days in mice, and 87–99% lower red-pigment concentration in old human tattoos than in fresh ones. Healed tattoos are therefore visibly lighter and slightly softer than the fresh photo.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, histology]** At 24 h, pigment is in phagosomes of keratinocytes, histiocytes, mast cells and fibroblasts. In the first month, before the basement membrane regenerates, some pigment is eliminated through the epidermis. — [Tattoos: risks and complications, clinical and histopathological approach (An Bras Dermatol 2024, ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0365059624000345)
- **[MEASURED, general wound healing]** Full re-epithelialisation typically takes 2–3 weeks, while epidermal maturation takes 8–12 weeks. — Same search cluster: [An Bras Dermatol 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0365059624000345?via=ihub); [Tattooed human in vitro skin model, healing progression (Sci Rep 2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-86813-2)
- **[MEASURED, in vivo human]** Two-photon FLIM: carbon black is exclusively intracellular in both fresh and old tattoos. Particles around the needle tracks were still visible 84 days after tattooing, showing delayed epidermal recovery. In 9-year-old tattoos, pigment sits in macrophages, mast cells and fibroblasts in the dermis, and also in keratinocytes, dendritic cells and basal cells of the continuously renewing epidermis. — [Dermatology 2023, "Tattoo Pigments Are Localized Intracellularly in the Epidermis and Dermis of Fresh and Old Tattoos" (Karger)](https://karger.com/drm/article/239/3/478/841419/Tattoo-Pigments-Are-Localized-Intracellularly-in); [PubMed 36787702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36787702/)
- **[MEASURED, mouse]** In SKH-1 mice, Pigment Red 22 measured 186 µg/cm² one day after tattooing and had dropped about 32% by day 42. Laser exposure at day 42 cut it by about another 51% compared with unexposed skin. — [Engel et al. 2010, Exp Dermatol 19:54-60](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26763404_Tattooing_of_skin_results_in_transportation_and_light-induced_decomposition_of_tattoo_pigments-a_first_quantification_in_vivo_using_a_mouse_model)
- **[MEASURED, human]** Freshly tattooed skin holds 0.60–9.42 mg/cm² of pigment (mean 2.53 mg/cm²). Red azo pigments (PR 22, PR 112) in biopsies of older human tattoos (n = 9) were 87–99% lower than in fresh tattoos, implying elimination during healing plus washout and breakdown over weeks to years. — [Engel et al. 2008, "Modern tattoos cause high concentrations of hazardous pigments in skin"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5499461_Modern_tattoos_cause_high_concentrations_of_hazardous_pigments_in_skin); [Lehner et al. 2011, JEADV (PubMed 21349116)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21349116)
- **[MEASURED, cosmetic tattoo]** NAC tattoos changed significantly between immediately after and 1 week, 1→3 months, 3→6 months and 6→12 months, with fading slowing but continuing to 12 months. — [PMC11288766](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11288766/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Silver/milky skin: a thin, semi-opaque new epidermis gives a hazy, washed-out look. It is not ink loss. It usually appears after peeling (about weeks 2–3, prominent in weeks 3–4) and clears over about 2–4 months, sooner on thin skin and later on large pieces. Colours look most muted and lines softest in this window, so artists advise waiting at least 4–6 weeks before judging touch-ups. — [xpaintattoo: The Milky Phase](https://xpaintattoo.com/blogs/news/the-milky-phase-why-your-healing-tattoo-looks-cloudy-or-faded); [hilooktattoo: Cloudy Phase](https://hilooktattoo.com/blog/why-new-tattoo-looks-dull-cloudy-healing-phase); [reviewtattoo: Healing Stages Week-by-Week](https://reviewtattoo.com/guides/tattoo-healing-stages/)

### Inferences
- **Suggested renderer states** (parameters inferred from the above, not measured on screen):
  - **Fresh (day 0–3):** full saturation and darkness. Add an erythema halo (raised a\*, a pink-red radial band a few mm around the lines) and slight gloss from ointment or plasma.
  - **Peeling / silver skin (weeks 2–6):** apply a semi-opaque whitish veil over the ink (lower contrast, slight lightening and blur).
  - **Healed / settled (about 2–3 months+):** veil gone. Ink about 5–15% lighter in luminance than fresh, with slightly reduced chroma and a few tens of µm of edge softening.
  - The 5–15% figure is an inference anchored on the NAC 9% luminance number. The pigment mass loss (32% in mice at 6 weeks, 87–99% in humans over years) is much larger than the visible change, because optical density saturates: black can lose a lot of pigment and still look black.
- Client copy can honestly say: "Fresh photos show the tattoo at its darkest. Healed tattoos are typically a bit lighter and softer, and colours keep fading slowly through the first year."

### Gaps
- No measured line-width change between fresh and healed (e.g. µm of edge softening at 6 weeks) was found.
- No quantitative erythema data (a\* over time) for tattoos was found. The redness duration figures available are practitioner-only.

## 3. Aging: line spread, fading by colour, UV, minimum detail and spacing rules

### Takeaway
Ink persists because dermal macrophages capture, die, release and recapture pigment, so it stays roughly in place but slowly migrates deeper and sideways. No peer-reviewed study gives "mm of line spread per decade". Practitioner rules cluster around ≥ 2 mm between parallel fine lines (≈ 1/16–1/8 in), gaps ≥ 1.5× line width, and minimum letter heights of about 5–6 mm (block about 1/4 in, script about 1/2 in). Yellow and red azo pigments are the most UV-labile.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, mouse]** Tattoo pigment in the dermis is held exclusively by dermal macrophages. When they die, the released particles, too big to drain, are recaptured by neighbouring or newly recruited monocyte-derived macrophages. Repeated capture–release–recapture cycles explain long-term persistence, and they also explain why laser-freed pigment is often recaptured. — [Baranska et al. 2018, J Exp Med 215:1115](https://rupress.org/jem/article/215/4/1115/42419/Unveiling-skin-macrophage-dynamics-explains-both); [EurekAlert summary](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/604373)
- **[MEASURED, review/patent literature]** Over long periods pigment gradually moves into the deeper (reticular) dermis, giving a faded, blurred look. Smaller particles reach deeper and drain to lymph nodes; synchrotron XRF found the same pigment species in skin and regional lymph nodes in 2 of 4 tattooed donors. — [Schreiver et al. 2017, Sci Rep (synchrotron XRF)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-11721-z); [Grant et al. 2015, Beilstein J Nanotechnol](https://www.beilstein-journals.org/bjnano/articles/6/120)
- **[MEASURED]** Pigment Red 22 was completely or almost completely destroyed in solution by 110 days of natural sunlight (UVB up to 8 h also cleaved it). Azo reds and yellows (e.g. yellow mono-azo C.I. 11741) are the most studied photolabile pigments. — [Engel et al. 2007, JDDG (PubMed 17610608)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17610608/); [Photostability and Breakdown Products of Pigments in Tattoo Inks (Karger, Curr Probl Dermatol)](https://karger.com/books/book/174/chapter/5111387/Photostability-and-Breakdown-Products-of-Pigments); [Systematic review of degradation products, J Expo Sci Environ Epidemiol 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41370-021-00364-y)
- **[MEASURED, human]** Red pigment concentration in older tattoos is 87–99% below fresh levels (see section 2). — [Lehner 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21349116)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Ink is said to spread sideways "like a drop of dye in water", forming a grey halo sometimes visible within weeks of healing. First softening appears around 2–3 years in sun-exposed areas, with clearly noticeable blurring around years 5–10. By year 10, fine script thickens and small gaps close. — [tattooswizard: How Fine Line Tattoos Actually Age](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/fine-line-tattoos-how-they-age); [certifiedtattoo](https://certifiedtattoo.com/blog/do-fine-line-tattoos-fade); [beforeyouink aging preview](https://beforeyouink.org/examples/fine-line-tattoo/)
- **[PRACTITIONER, unverified number]** "Lettering blurs as lines expand 10–20% in the first year." No primary source is given. — [aifortattoo / sortra search snippets](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/script-tattoo-fonts-minimum-size-line-weight-legibility-guide)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Spacing and size rules:
  - Never place parallel fine lines closer than about 2 mm.
  - Keep negative space at least 1.5× the line width.
  - Block or serif letters at least about 1/4 in (≈ 6 mm) tall (also phrased as "10 pt equivalent").
  - Standard script at least 1/2 in (≈ 12–13 mm), ornate script at least 1 in (≈ 25 mm).
  - "Most script letters at least 5–6 mm tall."
  - Sources: [tattooswizard](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/fine-line-tattoos-how-they-age); [aifortattoo minimum font size](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/script-tattoo-fonts-minimum-size-line-weight-legibility-guide); [sortra lettering size](https://sortra.com/how-big-should-a-tattoo-be-for-lettering/); [jeffjibran lettering guide](https://www.jeffjibran.com/tattoo-lettering-script-guide/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Artists suggest widening lines, spacing elements and simplifying rather than shrinking small designs. — [OSCO London: preventing fine-line blur](https://www.oscolondon.studio/post/how-to-prevent-fine-line-tattoos-from-blurring-over-time)
- **[PRACTITIONER / ink maker, unverified]** "UV exposure ... can cause up to 25% of color vibrancy to vanish within the first two years" and "UV ... roughly 90% of premature fading". These are marketing-blog figures with no primary source and should not be presented as data. — [Intenze Ink blog](https://intenzetattooink.com/blogs/news/preventing-tattoo-ink-fading-the-professional-guide-to-permanent-vibrancy); [Jotapas](https://www.jotapas.com/does-the-sun-fade-tattoos/)

### Inferences
- **Aging blur parameter (inferred, not measured):** model it as a Gaussian blur of the ink density map, with σ growing over time and saturating, e.g. σ(t) ≈ σ_heal + k·√t, plus slight overall darkening loss.
  - Practitioner reports that 2 mm gaps close by about 10–20 years suggest edge spread on the order of a few tenths of a mm per side per decade on typical skin. Tune k so that about 1 mm-wide gaps visibly fill by about 10 years on high-UV or high-friction sites.
  - Present this to clients as an illustration, not a prediction.
- **Colour-specific fade multipliers:** yellow, orange and red highest (azo, UV-labile); then light/pastel colours and white; then green and blue (phthalocyanines are generally regarded as more stable, though no source was fetched to confirm); black most stable.
- **Design-lint rules the app could enforce from the practitioner consensus:**
  - Warn when parallel-line spacing is under 2 mm at the chosen print size.
  - Warn when gaps are less than 1.5× the stroke width.
  - Warn when letter cap-height is under about 6 mm (block) or under about 12 mm (script).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed longitudinal measurement of tattoo line width over years (e.g. dermoscopy or OCT of the same tattoo at 0, 5 and 10 years) was found. The "1/8 inch" rule appeared only as practitioner lore, and the stated numbers range from 2 mm to 1/8 in (3.2 mm).
- No per-colour fade-rate constants (ΔE per year) in human skin were found. The available data are in-solution photolysis and one red-pigment biopsy series.

## 4. Optics: why black looks blue-grey, ink depth, epidermis effect

### Takeaway
Ink sits mostly in the upper-to-mid dermis. Depth estimates vary by method: about 0.3 mm (photothermal radiometry), about 0.6–2.2 mm (histology/reviews), and up to about 4 mm or deeper in amateur work. Viewed through scattering tissue, deep black reads blue-grey because red light penetrates and is absorbed by carbon while short wavelengths are back-scattered by the overlying dermis. The epidermis adds a melanin filter and a specular/sheen layer.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED, review/patent literature]** Pigment is commonly deposited about 1.5–2 mm below the surface; professional tattoos are injected at about 0.6–2.2 mm; ink can be found up to about 4 mm deep, and deeper in non-professional tattoos. — [ResearchGate: Tattoo ink nanoparticles in skin tissue and fibroblasts (Grant 2015) search summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/277190586_Tattoo_ink_nanoparticles_in_skin_tissue_and_fibroblasts)
- **[MEASURED, patent literature, conflicting]** One patent cluster states that professional tattoos sit at about 2–2.5 mm and amateur ones at about 1.5 mm. It also says professional work lies at an even mid-dermal depth while amateur depth varies from site to site. — [US patent 5217455 (laser treatment of pigmentations)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5217455); conflicts with in-vivo PPTR (next bullet).
- **[MEASURED, in vivo]** Pulsed photothermal radiometry in 5 volunteers found the laser-induced temperature peak of untreated tattoos "invariably" at about 300 µm subsurface, i.e. the effective optical depth of the ink layer. — [Milanič/Majaron et al., SPIE 2012 (ADS)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012SPIE.8207E..0GM/abstract); [Characterization of tattoos using PPTR and DRS (SPIE 2020)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339370532_Characterization_of_tattoos_in_human_skin_using_pulsed_photothermal_radiometry_and_diffuse_reflectance_spectroscopy)
- **[MEASURED]** Two-photon FLIM imaged tattoo pigment in vivo down to the reticular dermis. — [Dermatology 2023](https://karger.com/drm/article/239/3/478/841419/Tattoo-Pigments-Are-Localized-Intracellularly-in)
- **[MEASURED]** Ink is a suspension of nanoparticles (often under 100 nm) which, after injection, form interstitial aggregates of about 140–180 µm before being engulfed by fibroblasts and macrophages. — [Grant et al. 2015, Beilstein J Nanotechnol (search summary)](https://www.beilstein-journals.org/bjnano/articles/6/120)
- **[MEASURED, optics]** The absorption coefficient of black tattoo ink decreases monotonically with wavelength and has no distinct peaks, i.e. it is spectrally near-flat or slightly blue-weighted. A typical skin model in this literature uses μs = 24.95 mm⁻¹, μa = 0.31 mm⁻¹, g = 0.794. In ex-vivo porcine skin, tattooed and untattooed diffuse-reflectance spectra behave as two-layer at 500–600 nm and as single-layer at 600–850 nm, so red/NIR light "sees through" to the ink layer differently from green. — [Rudraiah et al. 2022, J Biophotonics](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jbio.202200003); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358063093_Diffused_reflectance_measurements_to_detect_tattoo_ink_location_in_skin_using_the_crossover_point)
- **[MEASURED, clinical]** Dermatology describes deep-dermal pigment appearing bluish from the Tyndall effect, as in traumatic tattoos and dermal melanocytosis. — [Traumatic tattoos mimicking pigmented tumours on videodermoscopy (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11626363/); [Wikipedia: Tyndall effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyndall_effect)
- **[PRACTITIONER / ink maker]** Black "turning blue/green" is blamed on poorly milled or inconsistent carbon, deeper placement, and fading that reveals the blue or green toners in some black formulas. — [Intenze: What is Ink?](https://intenzetattooink.com/blogs/news/what-is-ink-the-ultimate-guide-to-tattoo-pigments-and-permanence-in-2026); [Tattlab: Best Black Tattoo Ink 2026](https://www.tattlab.com.au/blogs/journal/best-black-tattoo-ink-2026-the-professional-artist-s-guide-to-saturation-and-stability)

### Inferences
- **Renderer:** treat black ink as a strong but not total absorber placed under a scattering dermal layer. A cheap approximation:
  - healed_black = mix(skin_color, ink_color, coverage), with ink_color a slightly blue-shifted dark grey rather than #000.
  - Use something like sRGB (35–55, 40–60, 50–70) on light skin, darkening and shifting toward neutral as melanin rises. This range is an inference, not measured.
  - Increase the blue shift with "age" and "depth" sliders.
- Because the ink sits below a scattering layer, the ink edge should be optically blurred even when fresh. A sub-surface blur of tens to a few hundred µm (on the order of the 0.3 mm ink depth) is physically plausible.
- Highlights and pores belong to the epidermis above the ink. The renderer should keep skin specular, normal and pore detail on top of the tattoo and never paint the tattoo over specular.

### Gaps
- No published absorption/scattering spectra (μa(λ), μs′(λ)) per tattoo colour were obtainable. The Springer colour-appearance paper and a ResearchGate figure ("absorption and reduced scattering coefficient as a function of ink concentration") likely contain them, but neither could be fetched.
- Depth estimates conflict (0.3 mm by PPTR versus 0.6–2.5 mm in reviews and patents). These measure different things: the optical centroid versus total histological extent.

## 5. Placement: stretch, distortion, movement, and fast-fading areas

### Takeaway
The only formal, expert-weighted placement data is the Kirby-Desai laser-removal scale (head/neck 1 → distal extremity 5), which tracks vascular and lymphatic clearance. Practitioner consensus: hands, fingers, feet and lips fade and blow out fastest because of thin skin, friction and turnover. Stomach, ribs, hips, inner arm, breasts and thighs distort most with weight change and pregnancy. Joints (elbow, knee) and the torso distort with movement.

### Cited Findings
- **[CLINICAL SCALE]** Kirby-Desai location points: head and neck 1; upper trunk 2; lower trunk 3; proximal extremity 4; distal extremity 5. The rationale is blood and lymphatic supply (head and neck have the most lymph nodes and vascular supply; proximal extremities have more lymphatic supply than distal). A total score below 4 suggests about 1–3 sessions, and above 15 suggests 10 or more. — [Kirby et al. 2009, J Clin Aesthet Dermatol (PMC2923953)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2923953/); [Astanza explainer](https://astanzalaser.com/the-kirby-desai-scale-for-laser-tattoo-removal/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Hand, finger and palm-side skin regenerates faster and sees more friction and washing, so ink fades or blurs quickly. Ink "sets differently" and blows out more easily. Fine and small work is most vulnerable, and touch-ups are frequent. One studio claims finger tattoos hold 6–12 months before visible fading. — [The Rose Ink and Art: hand tattoos](https://www.theroseinkandart.com/blog/the-truth-about-hand-tattoos-why-they-fade-and-why-artists-prefer-not-to-do-small-designs); [Oracle Tattoo Gallery: finger tattoos](https://oracletattoogallery.com/when-will-a-finger-tattoo-start-to-blur-and-fade/); [OSCO London: do hand tattoos fade](https://www.oscolondon.studio/tattoo-aftercare-1/do-hand-tattoos-fade)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Stretch- and weight-sensitive areas: stomach (pregnancy, bloating, weight, loose skin), hips, inner upper arm, chest and breasts, thighs, lower back, ribs. Movement-sensitive areas for fine line: fingers, ankles, elbows, knees, ribs. — [cangguinkclub: Do Tattoos Stretch?](https://cangguinkclub.com/blog/tattoos-stretch/); [sortra: placement for weight changes](https://sortra.com/tattoo-placement-for-weight-changes/); [Think Tank Tattoo: tattoos on abs](https://www.thinktanktattoo.com/post/tattoos-on-abs)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** "Torso crunch trap": a stencil placed straight while the client stands distorts when they sit or twist. Artists stencil in the relevant posture and may reshape or resize the design to follow muscle flow. Geometric, mandala, ornamental and strict-symmetry designs show distortion most, while organic or floral designs hide it. — [sortra: style and placement](https://sortra.com/how-tattoo-style-affects-placement/); [Skin Illustrations placement guide](https://skinillustrations.net/blog/tattoo-placement-guide-pros-cons-and-pain-levels/); [burnedhearts placement guide](https://burnedhearts.com/blog/tattoo-placement-ideas)
- **[PRACTITIONER, dubious]** One laser-removal blog claims ink deposited past about 1.2–1.8 mm "is absorbed radially at 0.5 mm/hour" by fat, with halos visible in 72 h. No source is given, and the claim is implausible as stated. Do not use it. — [poliinternational blog](https://poliinternational.com/blog/tiktok-s-viral-worst-tattoo-artist-saga-when-amate/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Blowout is ink placed too deep, into the loose hypodermis or along tissue planes, producing a blue-tinged blurry halo. It shows up early (days to weeks), unlike slow aging spread. — [Tattlab: Signs of an overworked tattoo](https://www.tattlab.com.au/blogs/journal/signs-of-an-overworked-tattoo-the-professional-guide-to-skin-integrity); [Studio Aureo: how deep do tattoo needles go](https://www.studioaureo.com/en/blog/2025/8/21/how-deep-do-tattoo-needles-go-a-complete-guide)

### Inferences
- **Per-region multipliers for the renderer's "aged" state:**
  - Fade/blur multiplier: hands, fingers, feet, lips high (e.g. 2–3×); forearm, shins and other sun-exposed distal limbs moderate-high; trunk, upper arm, thigh baseline. The ordering is directionally consistent with Kirby-Desai.
  - Distortion/pose sensitivity flag: stomach, ribs, hips, inner arm, chest, joints.
  - The multipliers are inferred, not measured.
- The app can show posture variants (arm bent or straight, seated torso) for flagged regions, which mirrors what artists check at stencil time.
- Blowout should not be part of a "normal aging" preview. It is a technique failure. If shown at all, offer it as an explicit "what poor technique looks like" example.

### Gaps
- No measured data on percentage distortion of a tattoo versus percentage skin strain or weight change was found.
- No measured regional fade rates in human skin were found. Kirby-Desai is a removal-response scale, so using it as a proxy for natural fading is an inference.

## 6. Published models and data usable in a renderer

### Takeaway
Usable published building blocks are generic skin optics (Jacques melanin absorption power law, roughly 50–60 µm epidermis over roughly 1 mm dermis, typical μs/μa/g), Monte Carlo and inverse-MC multi-layer models of tattooed skin, and pigment-load data (about 2.5 mg/cm²). No ready-made per-colour tattoo ink μa/μs′ table could be retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEASURED / model]** Jacques: melanosome absorption μa,mel = 6.6×10¹¹ · λ^−3.33 cm⁻¹ (λ in nm), e.g. 230 cm⁻¹ at 694 nm, 170 cm⁻¹ at 755 nm and 55 cm⁻¹ at 1064 nm. Epidermal absorption μa,epi = f_mel·μa,mel + (1 − f_mel)·μa,baseline. A 60 µm epidermis is the convention for stating f_mel. The epidermis (about 50 µm) is thin compared with the dermis (about 900 µm). — [OMLC Skin Optics Summary (Jacques 1998)](https://omlc.org/news/jan98/skinoptics.html); [OMLC Optical Absorption of Melanin](https://omlc.org/spectra/melanin/); [Jacques Skin Optics PDF copy](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Laurent-Brunel-3/post/When_I_press_an_LED_against_a_finger_light_travels_completely_through_1-2_cm_Can_diffusion_theory_be_used_to_model_this_observation/attachment/59d61f7979197b807797dea5/AS%3A284833398968322%401444920982739/download/Jacques+Skin+Optics+Summary.pdf)
- **[MEASURED / model]** Example skin parameters used in tattoo light-transport work: μs = 24.95 mm⁻¹, μa = 0.31 mm⁻¹, g = 0.794 (10 mm slab). Black ink μa falls monotonically with wavelength. Colour of tattooed skin has been predicted by Monte Carlo from measured skin and dye optical spectra, and inverse Monte Carlo on a 3-layer tattooed-skin model has been used to track tattoo changes. — [Rudraiah 2022, J Biophotonics](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jbio.202200003); [Springer: colour appearance of tattoo models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02348128); [ResearchGate figure: μa and μs′ vs ink concentration](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Absorption-and-reduced-scattering-coefficient-as-a-function-of-ink-concentration_fig8_6984221)
- **[MEASURED]** Pigment load: 0.60–9.42 mg/cm² (mean 2.53) in fresh tattoos. — [Engel 2008](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5499461_Modern_tattoos_cause_high_concentrations_of_hazardous_pigments_in_skin)
- **[MEASURED]** An in-vivo optical method (PPTR plus diffuse reflectance) was used to track laser removal objectively in volunteers. It showed ink content falling and the remaining ink layer appearing deeper after each treatment at ≥ 3 J/cm². — [Golmajer Zima et al. 2023, Lasers Surg Med (PubMed 37655731)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37655731); [SPIE 2012 PPTR](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012SPIE.8207E..0GM/abstract)

### Inferences
- A practical real-time approximation for the existing shader, since full MC is unnecessary:
  1. Composite the ink albedo into the dermal diffuse layer, not on top of the epidermis.
  2. Multiply by melanin transmission T(λ)², evaluated per RGB channel at about 610/550/465 nm with the Jacques power law and the app's existing melanin parameter.
  3. Blur the ink mask by a depth-dependent kernel (σ ≈ 0.1–0.3 mm fresh/healed, growing with age).
  4. Shift black toward blue-grey with age and depth.
  5. Keep epidermal specular, roughness and normals unchanged above the ink.
- This is consistent with the physics in sections 1 and 4. The constants are tuning values, not measurements.

### Gaps
- Per-colour tattoo-pigment μa(λ) and μs′(λ) spectra (carbon black, TiO₂ white, azo reds and yellows, phthalocyanine blues and greens) were not obtainable. The full texts of the Springer colour-appearance paper, the Rudraiah 2022 paper and the 2026 J Clin Med review ("Tattoo Pigment In Situ as a Determinant of Laser Treatment Response", MDPI) are the best leads but were blocked.
- No open dataset of calibrated photos of the same design across skin tones and ages was found.
