# Human body and skin asset licensing for a browser-shipped (three.js) tattoo preview

Research date: 2 Oct 2026. Context: the app ships GLB meshes and textures to the browser on a Shopify page, so any visitor can technically download them (DevTools / network tab). The app's policy so far is MIT / Apache / BSD / ISC / CC0 only.

**Method and access caveat (read first).** The sandbox's egress proxy blocked direct fetches of almost every primary page: unrealengine.com, metahuman.com, fab.com, forums.unrealengine.com, cgchannel.com, renderpeople.com, 3dscanstore.com, texturing.xyz, digitalrealitylab.com, humanscanrepository.com, makehumancommunity.org, huggingface.co, sketchfab.com, tuxfamily.org. Only raw.githubusercontent.com (public repos) and one Epic EULA PDF mirror on s3.amazonaws.com could be read in full. So:
- **"Verbatim"** marks text I read in full from the primary file (GitHub raw files, the Epic PDF).
- **"Search excerpt"** marks wording returned by the search engine from the cited page. It is usually the page's own sentence, but I could not check it against the full page, its section number or its date. Treat it as a strong lead, not a checked quote.
- Before launch, someone with normal web access should open each URL marked "search excerpt", save a dated PDF of the page, and confirm the wording.

## 1. MetaHuman (Epic Games): current licence after the June 2025 change, and whether a MetaHuman can ship as a GLB in a three.js page

### Takeaway
Since MetaHuman 5.6 (June 2025), MetaHumans made in UE 5.6+ fall under the standard Unreal Engine EULA. Epic now explicitly allows them "with any engine or creative software" and treats exported character assets as royalty-free "Non-Engine Products". Under $1M a year in revenue it costs nothing; above that you need UE seats at about $1,850 per seat per year. I found no clause in the current MetaHuman/UE terms that says, in so many words, "you must stop end users extracting a MetaHuman". I could not read the current EULA in full, though, and two things make shipping a downloadable GLB a legal grey area: the older "UE-Only Content" wording that still appears on metahuman.com/license, and the Fab Standard License's anti-extraction rule (which covers Fab-bought MetaHuman add-ons). The licence is also Epic's proprietary EULA, which is outside the app's MIT/Apache/BSD/ISC/CC0 policy. The default MetaHuman body has underwear baked into the texture and no genitals. Epic has no glTF export, so the path is UE → FBX → Blender → GLB.

### Cited Findings
**Licence change (dates and scope)**
- MetaHuman left Early Access in June 2025. MetaHuman Creator, Mesh to MetaHuman and MetaHuman Animator are now built into Unreal Engine 5.6 — [MetaHuman news: "MetaHuman leaves Early Access…"](https://www.metahuman.com/news/metahuman-leaves-early-access-with-a-feature-packed-new-release) (search excerpt); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/metahuman-just-broke-free-from-unreal-engine-5-why-everyone-can-now-create-lifelike-characters) (search excerpt)
- "new licensing options in the Unreal Engine EULA enable MetaHumans to be used with any engine or creative software" — [metahuman.com/license](https://www.metahuman.com/license) (search excerpt). The same page's FAQ answers "Yes, MetaHuman characters and animation can be used outside of Unreal Engine… The Unreal Engine EULA provides for MetaHuman characters and animation to be used across all engines and creative software, subject to applicable terms" (search excerpt).
- "MetaHuman is included as part of your standard Unreal Engine license… free to use for anyone making under $1 million USD in revenue and Unreal Engine standard terms apply above that threshold" — [metahuman.com/license](https://www.metahuman.com/license) (search excerpt)
- For MetaHuman with UE 5.6 and later the UE EULA applies. The old MetaHuman Creator EULA still covers the cloud MetaHuman Creator on UE 5.5 and earlier — [UE EULA change log](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula-change-log/unreal) and [MetaHuman Creator EULA](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula/mhc) (search excerpts)
- The June 2025 EULA change log (search excerpts): it updated the EULA "so MetaHuman characters and animation can be used in any engine, such as Unity or Godot, as well as creative software like Maya, Houdini, and Blender"; it "deleted content restrictions specific to Metahumans in Metahuman Content Addendum"; and it added "an exception… to the restriction in 5(c)(iii) to permit UE-Only Content to be used or accessed by third parties for the purpose of creating content for distribution to the public." It also added a new Section 17 on AI that forbids using "NoAI Content" in datasets for, or as inputs to, generative AI programs — [UE EULA change log](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula-change-log/unreal); [Content EULA change log](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/eula-change-log/content)
- Trade press (June 2025): MetaHuman characters and animations count as "non-engine products", so they can be used in Unity or Godot games without Epic's 5% royalty. Characters and clothing can now be sold on Fab. Studios over $1M a year need UE seats "which currently cost $1,850/year". MetaHumans may be used in "workflows that incorporate artificial intelligence technology" but not to train or improve AI models — [CG Channel, Jun 2025](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/06/you-can-now-sell-metahumans-or-use-them-in-unity-or-godot/) (search excerpt); [Digital Production, 5 Jun 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/06/05/metahumans-graduate-ready-for-unity-godot-and-the-fab-cash-register/) (search excerpt: "under-$1 million outfits keep every cent, bigger studios need an $1,850 per-seat annual licence, and no 5% revenue cut applies when you export characters to other engines")
- Only characters made with MetaHuman Creator in UE 5.6 or later may be sold on Fab; characters from 5.5 and earlier cannot — [Fab support: Can I sell characters created with MetaHuman Creator on Fab?](https://support.fab.com/s/article/Can-I-sell-characters-created-with-MetaHuman-Creator-on-Fab?language=en_US); [Epic docs: Selling MetaHumans on Fab](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/selling-metahumans-on-fab?lang=en-US) (search excerpts)

**The "Non-Engine Product" clause (the clause that would allow shipping a GLB)**
- Verbatim, from the **Unreal Engine EULA for Creators (PDF dated 18 Oct 2021; an older version, kept here because its wording carried into the current EULA according to the search excerpts below)**, §1(A)(a): "Non-Engine Products - You may Distribute Non-Engine Products to any third party. 'Non-Engine Product' means a Product made using the Licensed Technology that does not include any Engine Code or any Paid Content Distributed in uncooked source format … and does not require any Engine Code … to run. For example, and subject to the foregoing, you may freely Distribute non-interactive linear media products … and asset files (other than UE-Only Content) developed or used with the Engine Code, including in products that use or rely on other video game engines." — [UE EULA for Creators PDF (2021) on s3](https://s3.amazonaws.com/EULA/04be6996-1cf1-4f50-9e75-b22db4680516-EULA.pdf)
- The current EULA, per search excerpt: a Non-Engine Product "(i) does not include any Engine Code, (ii) does not require any Engine Code to run, and (iii) does not include Starter Content in source format", with examples including "asset files such as character models and animations that you developed using Engine Code". Asset files "remain Non-Engine Products even if included in products that use or rely on other video game engines", and no royalty is owed on Non-Engine Products — [Unreal Engine EULA](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula/unreal) (search excerpt; I could not confirm the section number or the exact current wording)

**Possible conflicts and grey areas**
- metahuman.com/license still returns this text in search: "Downloaded Characters can be used with or without modifications in your Products as UE-Only Content as permitted under the terms applicable to UE-Only Content in the UE Agreement" and "Downloaded Characters are licensed as MetaHuman Content under the terms of the Epic Content License Agreement" — [metahuman.com/license](https://www.metahuman.com/license) (search excerpt). This looks like the older MHC-era wording, or wording for Quixel Bridge "downloaded" characters, and it conflicts with the "any engine" statements above. **I could not resolve this conflict without reading the page.**
- Fab Standard License (which covers MetaHuman clothing, hair and skin add-ons bought on Fab, not the core MetaHuman itself): you "may Distribute a Project that incorporates Content as an included dependency to end users"; you "may not resell or redistribute the asset for free on a standalone basis"; and "when distributing a Project that incorporates Content as an included dependency, you must restrict end users from extracting or otherwise using Content outside of the Project" — [Fab Standard License / EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula?lang=en) (search excerpts; I could not confirm the section, which one excerpt calls Section 6 "Content Use Restrictions")

**Export path, formats and body**
- Exporting means bringing the MetaHuman into UE, exporting FBX from the Content Browser, then converting in Blender to glTF/GLB for three.js. I found no first-party glTF exporter for MetaHumans — [Medium: Exporting your MetaHuman from UE 5.6](https://medium.com/@little_michael101/exporting-your-metahuman-from-unreal-engine-5-6-for-metatailer-844c26ec5aee); [Blender Artists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/looking-for-an-up-to-date-tutorial-for-exporting-and-importing-unreal-engine-metahumans/1596355) (search excerpts). Quixel Bridge was the pre-5.6 download route, per the same excerpts.
- Default MetaHumans have "underwear painted on the body materials rather than being separate meshes". Polygons hidden by selected clothing are removed on export, so removing all clothing first keeps the full body. Epic stated "no current plan to release official MetaHumans with genitals" — [Epic forum: Nudity in MetaHuman?](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/nudity-in-metahuman/212683); [Epic forum: Metahumans' body mesh depending on clothes](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/metahumans-body-mesh-depeding-on-clothes/1661087); [Epic forum: Metahumans – underwear](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/metahumans-underwear/238833) (search excerpts). Third-party "no underwear" MetaHuman body textures are sold on Fab, e.g. [3 NEW Metahuman Body Textures + Underwear](https://www.fab.com/listings/41a4c396-13ab-440c-ac45-c37addb5b25e), and nude add-ons are sold on itch.io ([primalbone](https://primalbone.itch.io/metahuman-nude-male-and-female)). Each has its own licence.

### Inferences
- **Probably allowed, but not fully checked:** an exported MetaHuman body built in UE 5.6+ seems to fit "asset files … including in products that use or rely on other video game engines". A three.js page is such a product. I found no MetaHuman-specific anti-extraction clause. **Not allowed:** pre-5.6 / MHC-EULA characters, which were UE-only.
- **Main risk:** the leftover "UE-Only Content" wording on metahuman.com/license, plus the Fab rule that "you must restrict end users from extracting…" for any Fab add-on (for example a nude skin texture bought on Fab). A plain GLB on a public CDN can be downloaded trivially, and draco/meshopt compression does not count as access control. If any Fab-licensed piece is in the GLB, the app is very likely in breach.
- **Policy fit:** the UE EULA is a proprietary contract (revenue thresholds, governing law, the AI clause, Epic's right to change terms). It is **not** MIT/Apache/BSD/ISC/CC0. Adopting it would break the app's stated policy and needs an explicit owner decision.
- **Technical fit:** MetaHuman topology and UVs are unrelated to MakeHuman/Anny. Using a MetaHuman means replacing Anny's body and morph system, not just swapping a texture. MetaHuman body textures (with underwear baked in) cannot be put onto Anny UVs without re-baking.

### Gaps
- I could not read the current UE EULA (unrealengine.com/eula/unreal) or the metahuman.com/license FAQ in full, so I have no confirmed section numbers or version dates. The UE-Only Content conflict is unresolved.
- I could not confirm current MetaHuman body poly counts or texture resolutions, or whether UE 5.7/5.8 added a first-party glTF/USD export for MetaHumans (a "MetaHuman 5.8 is now available" news post exists: https://www.metahuman.com/news/metahuman-5-8-is-now-available, not read).
- Whether Epic counts "web page that streams a GLB" as distributing "asset files" versus distributing extractable "Licensed Content": no Epic statement found. Contact Epic licensing for written confirmation.

## 2. Commercial scan vendors: Renderpeople, 3D Scan Store / Ten24, HumanScanRepository / Digital Reality Lab, TexturingXYZ

### Takeaway
Every paid vendor I checked allows games and real-time use but forbids redistribution. Renderpeople says explicitly that you may not make the 3D data available "in such a way that the 3D data can be downloaded, extracted, distributed or accessed by third parties as single files". A plain GLB/texture served to a browser is exactly that, so none of these is safe for a downloadable three.js asset without a negotiated licence. All are outside the app's policy. The one notable exception is Digital Reality Lab's free **HSRD-100** dataset (CC BY 4.0), but those scans are clothed.

### Cited Findings
**Renderpeople**
- Renderpeople allows real-time rendering "for commercial or private use, such as for AR, VR, and XR applications as well as computer and video games". It forbids "Transfer, especially renting, reselling, lending, or sublicensing the 3D data… neither in their original form nor in parts or modified versions". It also states: "Customers are not allowed to make the 3D data available to third parties in such a way that the 3D data can be downloaded, extracted, distributed or accessed by third parties as single files." Other uses need "express written consent" or a separate licence — [Renderpeople General Terms & Conditions §4](https://renderpeople.com/general-terms-and-conditions/); [Renderpeople FAQ](https://renderpeople.com/faq/) (search excerpts)
- Products: Posed, Rigged, Animated and 4D People. Prices: Posed People Classic $29, New Gen $39, Posed People Plus $59 (300k and 100k poly versions, 8k textures downsampled from 16k RAW), Posed People Plus Ultimate Bundle $3,900 (was $5,900) — [Renderpeople Posed Plus](https://renderpeople.com/posed-plus/); [Renderpeople shop](https://renderpeople.com/3d-people/) (search excerpts). The range is clothed (including swimwear and workwear). I found no nude full-body product.
- Free models exist ([Free 3D People](https://renderpeople.com/free-3d-people/)) and are covered by the same terms (search excerpt; I could not confirm whether the free models carry any extra restriction).

**3D Scan Store (run by Ten24, Sheffield)**
- 3D Scan Store is "owned and run by Ten24 artists James Busby and Chris Rawlinson" — [3D Scan Store About](https://www.3dscanstore.com/about-us) (search excerpt)
- Zero-tolerance clause: scans are "for artistic and reference use only" and "no scan may be used, altered or otherwise in any overtly sexually explicit image, video, animated GIF or printed work regardless of the purchased licence" — [3D Scan Store Terms & Conditions / Licensing](https://www.3dscanstore.com/terms-and-conditions-licensing) (search excerpt)
- Prohibited uses include "resale or public sharing of the scan or any derivative in any 3D format", "integration of scan data into character maker programmes where results can be exported as 3D models", and, on the lower tiers, use "for final for-sale commercial projects such as games or movies" — same page (search excerpts; I could not confirm which tier each prohibition applies to)
- Three licence types. The "Business Commercial Project Licence" is the one for integrating into "a commercial, for sale product". Example: a 10× HD Head Scan Pack at £449 base, with the Business Commercial Single Project licence costing **+£1,943** — [3D Scan Store Commercial Customer Info](https://www.3dscanstore.com/studios-and-commercial-customer-information); [10 x HD Head Scan Pack 05](https://www.3dscanstore.com/hd-head-scans/hd-head-bundles/10xhd-head-scan-pack-05) (search excerpts)
- Nude anatomy content exists: a "female anatomy reference pack" of 19 reference poses in OBJ with 16k textures, plus 1 retopologised, cleaned A-pose with 16,384 × 16,384 TGA/JPG colour and 8K displacement and normal maps. "Retopologised animation ready body scans" come with 6 ZBrush subdivision levels, 16k maps and FBX exports — [3D Scan Store blog: New female anatomy reference pack](https://www.3dscanstore.com/blog/News/new-female-anatomy-reference-pack/); [3D Body Models](https://www.3dscanstore.com/3d-body-models) (search excerpts; I did not find prices for these packs)
- "Ten24" products on TurboSquid carry TurboSquid's own licence tiers: Standard with $10,000 legal protection, Small Business +$99 ($250k), Enterprise +$229 ($1M), Agency +$429 (uncapped). The search engine attributed these to "Ten24", but they are TurboSquid's standard tiers — [TurboSquid Ten24 artist page](https://www.turbosquid.com/Search/Artists/Ten24) (search excerpt; flagged as possibly mis-attributed)

**HumanScanRepository / Digital Reality Lab (same company)**
- Licences: Personal Use (non-commercial; "redistribution, resale, or sublicensing of the models in any form is strictly prohibited"), Commercial Use ("video games, films, VR experiences, and tech demos"; "redistribution, resale, or sublicensing of the models outside the agreed commercial project is prohibited"), plus Academic, ML/AI training and Exclusive tiers — [HumanScanRepository Licensing Guide](https://humanscanrepository.com/licensing-guide/); [Digital Reality Lab blog: workflow & licensing](https://www.digitalrealitylab.com/blog/high-resolution-3d-scanning-of-humans-workflow-licensing/) (search excerpts)
- **HSRD-100** (released 2025): 100 photogrammetry scans (10 people × 10 poses, 50/50 male/female, ages 21–64, mixed ethnicities). LOD0 is about 1M polys with 16k textures, LOD1 about 100k with 8k, LOD2 about 10k with 4k "for real-time use". 112-camera rig. Outfits include "casual wear, business attire, sportswear, and formal clothing". Licence: **CC BY 4.0 — "free for both academic and commercial use with attribution"** — [Digital Reality Lab blog: HSRD-100](https://www.digitalrealitylab.com/blog/hsrd-100/); [Hugging Face: digitalrealitylab/HSRD-100](https://huggingface.co/datasets/digitalrealitylab/HSRD-100) (search excerpts)

**TexturingXYZ**
- Materials may be used in "computer games and other projects specifically permitted by the applicable license" and only as "incorporated into permitted end products". "You may not create and sell customer assets derived from TexturingXYZ Materials on their own". Buying does not transfer IP — [TexturingXYZ Terms & conditions / Licensing](https://texturing.xyz/pages/terms-of-service) (search excerpts)
- Tiers: a Personal Single User License (with a freelancer exception for "their own commercial work") and a Business Commercial Project License for "video games and films". Example price: $29.90 personal licence for MicroSkinFace#01, with volume discounts of 15–50% — same page (search excerpts). The products are mostly face and micro-skin detail maps, not full-body meshes.

### Inferences
- In a three.js app the GLB and texture URLs appear in the network tab and can be saved directly. That matches Renderpeople's "downloaded, extracted … as single files" prohibition, and very likely breaches the "public sharing of… any derivative in any 3D format" and "outside the agreed commercial project" clauses of 3D Scan Store and HumanScanRepository. Obfuscation or encryption in JavaScript does not reliably change that, because the browser must decode the data. **None of the paid vendors is usable without a written, negotiated web/real-time licence.**
- 3D Scan Store's ban on "integration into character maker programmes where results can be exported" may also catch a body-shape editor that offers screenshot/export features. Ask them before buying.
- **HSRD-100 is the only "real scan" source found that is legally shippable**, under CC BY 4.0 with attribution, which is outside the app's CC0-only policy (CC BY needs an attribution notice). The scans are clothed, posed, single-frame meshes with their own topology, so they suit at most forearm, hand or face skin patches. They are not a full-body skin for Anny.
- Prices put a single commercial body licence from 3D Scan Store at roughly £2,000+ (inferred from the head-pack example). Renderpeople individual models are cheap ($29–59), but their terms forbid exactly this use.

### Gaps
- Exact current tier wording and prices for 3D Scan Store full-body and anatomy packs; TexturingXYZ Business licence price; Renderpeople's custom "online/web" licence (they say "express written consent" is available, terms not public).
- I found no separate Ten24-branded licence apart from 3D Scan Store's and TurboSquid's.
- I could not confirm whether HSRD-100 includes any minimally clothed (swimwear or underwear) subjects, or whether the HF dataset card adds model-release or "no sensitive use" conditions on top of CC BY.

## 3. MakeHuman / MPFB2 CC0 skins (skins01, skins02, system skins) and fit with Anny's UVs

### Takeaway
MakeHuman and MPFB2 assets, including textures and the system skins, are CC0 1.0 (verbatim from the MPFB2 LICENSE). The official asset packs skins01 (natural female, about 99 MB) and skins02 (natural male, about 72 MB) are listed as CC0. All of them are painted for the MakeHuman base mesh (hm08) UV layout, which Anny inherits from MPFB2, so they are the only option that fits both the app's licence policy and its UVs without re-baking. I could not confirm resolution or exact map sets; they appear to be mostly diffuse-only PNGs of varying quality, and I found no confirmation that most include normal or specular maps.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim, MPFB2 `LICENSE.md`: assets are "any data contributing to the output from MPFB. This includes: The base mesh and proxies; Targets and modifiers; **Textures**; Clothes…; Rigs, poses and expressions…" and "These assets have been released under CC0 1.0 Universal… anyone can do whatever they want with it." Output: "the MakeHuman team makes no claim whatsoever over output such as: Exports to files (FBX, OBJ, DAE, MHX2...)… Renderings… Screenshots" — [makehumancommunity/mpfb2 LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/master/LICENSE.md)
- Verbatim, MPFB2 README: "MPFB 2.x requires a Blender version of at least 4.2." — [mpfb2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/master/README.md)
- MakeHuman system assets (base mesh, system skins) come from the `makehuman-assets` Git LFS repo, branch `master`, folder `base/` (verbatim from the downloader: `_git_official_assets_repo = "https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman-assets.git"`, `_git_official_assets_branch = "master"`) — [makehuman download_assets_git.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/makehuman/download_assets_git.py)
- The MakeHuman 1.1 system skin texture folder contains `young_lightskinned_female_diffuse.png` (4.1 MB), `…_diffuse2.png` (3.5 MB) and `…_diffuse3.png` (3.7 MB) — [tuxfamily mirror: makehuman/assets/1.1/base/skins/textures/](https://download.tuxfamily.org/makehuman/assets/1.1/base/skins/textures/) (search excerpt; I could not list the whole folder, read pixel dimensions, or see whether normal or specular maps sit beside them)
- skins01: "natural female skins shared under CC0", download about 99 MB. Includes community skins such as blindsaypatten_uniform_skin_texture, bobby_03_young_female_hairless, callharvey3d_midtoned_female, cutoff3d_indian_female_enhanced, cutoff3d_indian_female_skin, darthfurby_caucasian_female, flower-angel_red_head_skin — [MakeHuman skins01](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/skins01.html) (search excerpt)
- skins02: "natural male skins shared under CC0", about 72 MB, with skins by jartur69, ken1138, Mindfront, OnlyTheGhosts and others — [MakeHuman skins02](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/skins02.html) (search excerpt)
- MakeHuman docs: skin textures are "normal images (usually PNG files) with large dimensions between 1024x1024 and 8192x8192", and each needs an MHMAT text descriptor — [MakeHuman: How can I create skin textures?](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/faq/how_can_i_create_skin_textures.html) (search excerpt)
- MPFB's "Enhanced skin" material builds on the selected diffuse texture and adds procedural colour and bump, which suggests the packs are mostly diffuse-only — [MPFB docs: Enhanced skin](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/docs/materials/skinv1.html) (search excerpt)
- Third-party "Skins Vol.0 / Vol.1" on itch.io are **CC-BY**, not CC0 — [skins vol0](https://rpatterson-smokeworks.itch.io/skins-vol0); [skins vol1](https://rpatterson-smokeworks.itch.io/skins-vol1) (found in earlier project research)
- The project's own licence log confirms that Anny's `data/mpfb2` (MakeHuman base mesh, targets, rig, UVs) is CC0 1.0 and that Anny ships only partial MakeHuman textures (face, lips, eyelids, ears, areolae, nails, sss) — `/home/user/Tattoo/docs/licenses.md`; [naver/anny data/mpfb2/textures](https://github.com/naver/anny/tree/main/src/anny/data/mpfb2/textures)

### Inferences
- **UV fit:** MakeHuman skins (system or asset pack) are authored for the hm08 base-mesh default UV set. Anny's default `anny` topology is the MPFB2 base mesh with its UVs (per the project licence log), so these textures should map directly. Risks: Anny strips eye backs, sockets, mouth and tongue and straightens elbows, which does not affect UVs; but some community skins in skins01/02 target an alternative UV set or proxy topology. Check each MHMAT's `diffuseTexture` and load-test before adopting.
- **Licence fit:** CC0 fits the app's policy exactly. Keep a record of the pack page and date, because community uploaders asserted CC0 (inherited risk: a contributor might have painted from a non-CC0 photo source).
- **Quality expectation:** the 4 MB PNG sizes suggest roughly 2k-class diffuse maps (inference, not verified). Expect painted or older photo-sourced diffuse maps without calibrated normal, roughness or SSS maps. Tattoo realism will still depend on the app's own detail-normal/pore layer.
- The skins01 "hairless" and "uniform" entries are the most useful for tattoos, because they avoid baked body hair or underwear. This is inferred from the names; I did not view the textures.

### Gaps
- Pixel dimensions, and whether normal, specular or roughness maps exist, for the system skins (young_lightskinned_*, young_darkskinned_*, etc.) and each skins01/02 item. The makehuman-assets repo and the MakeHuman sites were blocked. Fix: `git lfs` clone `makehuman-assets/base/skins` and run `identify *.png`.
- Whether any skins01/02 textures are nude (genitals and nipples painted) versus underwear-baked.

## 4. CC0 / CC-BY photogrammetry body scans, and research datasets to exclude

### Takeaway
I found no CC0 full-body nude photogrammetry scan that suits a commercial product. Open options are: a few CC-BY full-body scans on Sketchfab (clothed, hobby quality, attribution required), Polycam Explore captures (CC BY 4.0), HSRD-100 (CC BY 4.0, clothed, high quality), and CC0 tiling 2k skin materials ("Human Materials 1 (CC0)" on Sketchfab). The well-known research body datasets (SMPL/SMPL-X, THuman, AGORA/RenderPeople academic) are non-commercial and must be excluded.

### Cited Findings
- Sketchfab CC-BY full-body photogrammetry examples: "Full Body Scan – Clean Pose" and "A-Pose – Male Full Body 3D Scan" (OverseerThuror), "Full body 3D scan" (FabLab Budapest), "Character Scan Photogrammetry" (phone-captured) — [Sketchfab: Full Body Scan – Clean Pose](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/full-body-scan-clean-pose-ae9559912f6240a8a87057911b69edd0); [A-Pose – Male](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/a-pose-male-full-body-3d-scan-78c2d136f91c46a8a696d5bacfde2daf); [FabLab Budapest](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/full-body-3d-scan-c531fb928a5c408286a1ab7d9e18958e); [Character Scan Photogrammetry](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/character-scan-photogrammetry-e62179794eae4e74b06a8f8f5153c0cc) (search excerpts; I did not check clothing, resolution or each licence version)
- "Human Materials 1 (CC0)" on Sketchfab: "seamless 2k textures" with colour, AO, roughness, normal and displacement; "free to use with no attribution needed"; made because the author "could only find a few public domain textures for human skin and faces" — [Sketchfab: Human Materials 1 (CC0)](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/human-materials-1-cc0-be48a7526b304914b7c9f45c289b2ecf) (search excerpt)
- Polycam: "All savable content on Polycam's Explore page is licensed under the Creative Commons 4.0 license… CC BY 4.0". Polycam's ToS lets users commercialise their own captures "provided that you provide attribution to the Company for any public display of any such models" — [Polycam: Usage Rights and Ownership](https://learn.poly.cam/hc/en-us/articles/27646853145748-Usage-Rights-and-Ownership); [Polycam Terms of Service](https://poly.cam/legal/terms-of-service) (search excerpts)
- HSRD-100: CC BY 4.0, clothed — see Question 2.
- **Exclude:** THuman2.0 is "available for non-commercial research purposes only… prohibited, including incorporation in a commercial product… web services… mobile applications" — [THuman2.0-Dataset GitHub](https://github.com/ytrock/THuman2.0-Dataset) (search excerpt). SMPL / SMPL-X: "non-commercial scientific research… any use for commercial purposes, prohibited", with commercial licences sold via Meshcapade — [SMPL Model License](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [SMPL-X Model License](https://smpl-x.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html) (search excerpts). AGORA is built from RenderPeople scans with registration-gated terms — [AGORA](https://agora.is.tue.mpg.de/) (search excerpt). The project already excludes Anny's optional `smpl`/`smplx` topologies, which come from Anny's `noncommercial.zip` (`/home/user/Tattoo/docs/licenses.md`).

### Inferences
- CC-BY scans are legally shippable with an attribution notice in the app's About text and source credits. That needs a policy exception, since the app is CC0-only today. Polycam's ToS "attribution to the Company" condition (for a capturer's own models) shows that platform terms can add conditions on top of CC.
- Hobby Sketchfab scans have unknown model releases. A CC licence covers the uploader's copyright, but **not** the scanned person's likeness or publicity rights. For a real person's body used as a commercial product model, this is a separate legal risk (inference; no source found on these specific uploads).
- The realistic open path for photoreal skin on Anny is: CC0 MakeHuman diffuse (Q3), plus CC0 tiling detail maps (Human Materials 1, 2k), plus the app's own procedural pores. Re-projecting a CC-BY scan onto Anny UVs is possible but means re-baking and taking on attribution and likeness risk.

### Gaps
- I did not find Smithsonian Open Access (CC0) or Thingiverse human-body photogrammetry suitable as skin. The Smithsonian's CC0 3D items are mostly objects and sculptures; I did not run a dedicated search, so treat this as unverified.
- Resolution, clothing and model-release status of each Sketchfab CC-BY scan was not checked (Sketchfab blocked).

## 5. Microsoft Rocketbox (MIT) and similar permissive avatar libraries

### Takeaway
Rocketbox is genuinely MIT-licensed (fits the policy). It has 115 rigged, low-poly, **clothed** avatars with 2048² head and body textures (colour, normal and specular). Clothing is modelled into the mesh, there is no nude body, and the textures are hand-painted or photo-sourced game-style. So it is of little use for a skin-visible tattoo preview, beyond hands, faces or forearms on short-sleeve characters. It does not share MakeHuman UVs.

### Cited Findings
- Verbatim, README: "The Microsoft Rocketbox Avatar library consists of 115 characters and avatars fully rigged and with high definition… relatively low-poly meshes". Updates: "12/2020: Updated license to MIT"; "The library of avatars is now released under MIT License"; facial blendshapes (2/2022: 15 visemes, 48 FACS), 417 animations (4/2022), ARKit blendshapes (6/2022). Poly levels: "hipoly", "midpoly", "lowpoly", "ultralowpoly". Delivered as FBX with 3ds Max materials — [microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox/master/README.md)
- LODs: hipoly about 10,000 triangles, midpoly about 5,000, lowpoly about 2,500, ultralowpoly about 500. Textures "usually… 2048x2048 pixels, with one separate texture for the head and another one for the body", with "color, normal and specular maps" — [Gonzalez-Franco et al., Frontiers in VR 2020 (PDF)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Rocketbox-Library-Rigged-Avatars.pdf) (search excerpt)
- "Clothing is hard modeled into their bodies, not being able to be separated or swapped" — [PSU review of Rocketbox](https://sites.psu.edu/alexfatemi/2022/08/11/rocketbox_review/) (search excerpt)
- Anny (NAVER, Apache-2.0 code + CC0 MPFB2 assets) is the app's current body. Its licence chain is documented in `/home/user/Tattoo/docs/licenses.md`, and the Anny paper is at [arXiv 2511.03589](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03589v1).

### Inferences
- Rocketbox's licence is fine, but its content is wrong for this use: clothed, about 10k triangles, 2k textures split between head and body. Tattoo areas (back, chest, thighs, ribs) are covered by clothing geometry. The skin-only parts (hands, face) could serve as colour references at most.
- MakeHuman/MPFB2 (CC0) is already the best permissive full-body nude base, and the app uses it via Anny. No other permissive library found in this research beats it for skin visibility.

### Gaps
- I did not check other permissive avatar sets (e.g. Ready Player Me, which is proprietary; VRoid sample models, which carry custom licences; Mixamo characters, under Adobe terms). None appeared to be both permissive and unclothed in what I found.
