## 1. Pricing models and typical US price ranges (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
US artists combine four models: a **shop minimum** (about $100-$200) for small work, **flat/per-piece quotes** for small-to-medium pieces, an **hourly rate** (about $150-$250/hr for established artists, $80-$130 in small markets, $300-$400+ for top city artists) for large work, and **half-day/full-day session rates** (about $600-$3,000/day) for multi-session pieces. Size charts in guides are only rough anchors; real quotes come from the artist's estimate of **hours**.

### Cited Findings
**Models in use**
- Three main methods: hourly rate (most common for larger/complex pieces), flat rate (smaller, simpler designs), per-piece pricing — [Tattoo Studio Pro, "Tattoo Prices in 2026" [vendor]](https://tattoostudiopro.com/tattoo-prices/)
- Typical structure: you pay the shop minimum regardless of size, and artists then charge hourly after the first hour — [Hustle Butter pricing guide](https://www.hustlebutter.com/blogs/news/how-much-do-tattoos-cost)
- Real studio example, Fatty's Tattoos (DC/MD multi-location): tattoos of 7+ hours are charged at least $200/hr; anything under 7 hours is **priced by the piece** "at a rate higher than the hourly rate"; base minimum $100; **minimum $150 for head, face, neck, elbows, knees, hands and feet**; preparation time (research, drafting) is billed — [Fatty's Tattoos pricing page](https://www.fattystattoos.com/tattoo-pricing)
- Real studio example, Studio City Tattoo (Los Angeles): shop minimum $120; day rate (6 hours) $1,000; $120 deposit for a normal appointment, $300 deposit for day-rate tattoos — [Studio City Tattoo pricing & deposit page](https://www.studiocitytattoo.com/tattoo-deposit-page/)
- Real studio example, Con Ink Tattoos: a "4 Hours (Half Day) Tattoo Session 12pm-4pm" is sold as a product for $600, described as a discount from $800 at the hourly rate — [Con Ink product page](https://www.coninktattoos.com/products/4-hours-half-day-tattoo-session-12pm-4pm)

### Inferences
- The number the artist actually reasons about is **estimated hours** (or sessions), multiplied by their rate and floored at the shop minimum. Size, placement, style and skin factors all feed into hours. A preview tool that outputs **physical dimensions plus a clear placement and style description** gives the artist what they need to estimate hours; it should not try to output dollar figures unless the artist configures their own rate rules.
- Placement-specific minimums (Fatty's: $150 for hands, feet, neck, elbows, knees, head, face) mean placement has to be captured as a **specific anatomical zone**, not just "arm" or "leg".
- Day and half-day packaging suggests the tool's quote output could usefully include "estimated sessions" as well as a dollar figure.

### Gaps
- No authoritative survey-based US price dataset (for example an industry association survey) was found; all size charts are vendor/SEO aggregations and disagree with each other by 2x or more.
- Specific prices for "palm-sized" and "3x3 in" pieces were not quoted anywhere individually; they fall inside the "small 2-4 in" band.
- Reddit r/tattooartists threads on quoting could not be retrieved (fetch blocked; search returned no Reddit hits).

## 2. Factors that change the quote

### Takeaway
Quote drivers, roughly in order: **size**, **detail/complexity and style** (realism > color > black and grey > simple linework), **placement difficulty** (ribs, hands, feet, neck, elbows, knees; often +20-30% or a higher minimum), **cover-up / scar / existing tattoos**, and **skin condition**. All of these work through extra hours or extra sessions.

### Cited Findings
- Placement: the same design can cost about twice as much on the ribs as on the forearm; ribs typically 20-30% more than the same design on an arm because of thin skin, breathing movement and pain; a medium rib piece runs about $600-$1,000 — [Tattoo Studio Pro, Cost by Body Part [vendor]](https://tattoostudiopro.com/tattoo-cost-by-body-part/); [Neebol body placement guide [vendor]](https://www.neebol.com/blogs/tattoo-business/tattoo-cost-by-body-placement-full-price-guide-chart)
- Hands and feet: $150-$400 for a hand piece **plus a likely touch-up session**; constant friction, sun and moisture make these fade and blur faster than almost anywhere else — same sources
- Neck: small side-neck piece starts around $200-$300; larger or back-of-neck work $500-$800+; positioning difficulty and a "visibility premium" — same sources
- Why: ribs, hands, feet and neck require working around curves, thin skin or high-movement areas, which slows the session — [Apprentice, Cost by Body Part [vendor]](https://useapprentice.com/blog/tattoo-cost-by-body-part-how-placement-affects-price)
- Real shop rule: higher minimum ($150 vs $100) for head, face, neck, **elbows, knees**, hands and feet — [Fatty's Tattoos](https://www.fattystattoos.com/tattoo-pricing)
- Complexity and color: simple black outline is cheapest, color is about 10-20% more expensive, photorealism is highest — search summary of pricing guides (indicative)
- Cover-ups usually cost more than fresh tattoos because the artist works around an existing image, faded pigment, scars or awkward placement — [Sortra, What Affects Tattoo Cost [SEO]](https://sortra.com/what-affects-tattoo-cost/); [Hyperinkers, cover-up cost](https://hyperinkers.com/blog/how-much-a-cover-up-tattoo-costs)
- Scar cover-ups: expect 2-3 sessions and possibly medical clearance — [Soulkraft Ink](https://www.soulkraftink.com/post/scar-cover-up-tattoo)
- Specialist scar/stretch-mark **camouflage** (skin-tone pigment, a separate service from artistic tattooing) is priced very differently: about $399-$799 per session, or $1,200-$4,600 total depending on area, size, texture and color; stretch-mark camouflage $150-$1,500 per session, usually several sessions — [Dermagrafix](https://dermagrafix.net/blog/scar-camouflage-tattoo-cost); [Leticia Marcelino](https://www.leticiamarcelino.com/post/how-much-does-tattoo-scar-camouflage-cost); [Aesthetik Artistry](https://www.aesthetikartistry.com/post/stretch-mark-scar-camouflage-how-much-is-it)
- Darker skin tones: not every artist is experienced with color on melanin-rich skin; recommended practice is to look for healed examples on similar skin tones and discuss color test patches; deep saturated colors (emerald, royal blue, red, plum, navy) work well, pastels and very light shades should be avoided — [Spooner's No Fun, tattooing darker skin tones](https://www.spoonersnofun.com/tattooing-darker-skin-tones)
- Prep time: Fatty's bills research and drafting time, so design complexity affects price before needling starts — [Fatty's Tattoos](https://www.fattystattoos.com/tattoo-pricing)

### Inferences
- A preview tool should capture **flags** that are hard to see from a design file: cover-up yes/no (plus a photo of the existing tattoo), scars/stretch marks in the area, existing nearby tattoos to work around, skin tone (affects color palette advice), and first tattoo yes/no.
- Elbow ditch, knee and hand placements are both pricier and more distorting on a 3D body, so the tool could flag "high-movement / difficult zone" automatically from where the design lands on the mesh.
- "Client body shape" was not cited by any source as an explicit price factor. It acts indirectly through the area to be covered (a sleeve on a larger arm is more square inches, so more hours) and through curvature.

### Gaps
- No source quantified price uplifts for loose or aged skin, sun damage or stretch marks inside a regular (non-camouflage) tattoo quote; artists say these matter, but I found no citable numbers.
- No reliable numbers on fine-line pricing versus traditional styles.

## 3. Information artists collect on quote/booking request forms

### Takeaway
Real forms consistently ask for: **contact info + age/ID**, **artist preference**, **idea description (subject/style)**, **placement**, **approximate size in inches**, **color vs black and grey**, **reference images (often 3-5)**, **cover-up / existing tattoos**, **budget**, **availability**, and they take a **non-refundable deposit** (about $100-$300) once approved. Several also ask for a **photo of the body area**, and a few ask about **skin type**. Health info (allergies, medical conditions) usually goes on the consent/waiver form at the appointment, not the quote form.

### Cited Findings
**Real studio examples**
- Monolith Studio (Brooklyn): booking form asks for desired size **in inches** and body location; non-refundable deposit to confirm — [Monolith FAQ](https://monolithstudio.com/faq)
- Studio OUCH!: three-part booking form including design details with sizing and placement options; non-refundable $100 deposit applied to the tattoo cost — [Studio OUCH! booking](https://www.ouch.studio/tattoos/booking)
- Cozy Tattoo Studio: request form asks desired size (inches) and placement; $100 deposit, non-refundable, applied to final cost — [Cozy policies](https://cozytattoostudio.com/policies)
- CTRL+ALT+Z Tattoo: booking form asks for **skin type**, placement and size; deposits non-refundable — [CTRL+ALT+Z information page](https://ctrlaltz.tattoo/information/)
- Bang Bang NYC: book by walk-in, phone, or a detailed email with design description, photo references and preferred dates — [Bang Bang FAQ](https://www.bangbangforever.com/faq)
- Porter (tattoo booking software) forms are described as capturing client age, preferred artist, tattoo details and photo references — [Porter online booking [vendor]](https://www.getporter.io/features/online-booking)
- Deposit examples: Studio City Tattoo $120 standard / $300 day-rate — [Studio City Tattoo](https://www.studiocitytattoo.com/tattoo-deposit-page/); $150 deposit at another studio (search snippet); see also [Club Tattoo booking policies](https://clubtattoo.com/blogs/tattoo-guide/essential-tattoo-booking-policies-you-need-to-know), [Whitebird Studio deposit policy](https://whitebirdstudio.tattoo/appointments-deposits-touch-up-policies/), [Think Tank Tattoo deposit policy](https://www.thinktanktattoo.com/post/tattoo-deposit-policy)

**Recommended form fields (software vendors / guides)**
- Placement, approximate size, whether it's a **cover-up or working around existing tattoos**; budget range; whether the client prefers **shorter sessions or long sessions**; reference-photo upload; client identity, age and contact details — [Bookedin, intake form guide [vendor]](https://bookedin.com/blog/tattoo-appointment-intake-forms-what-to-include/)
- Color or black-and-grey preference is a standard question, used for planning and quoting — [Venue, booking form questions + waiver template [vendor]](https://www.venue.ink/blog/tattoo-booking-form-questions-waiver-template)
- Client advice: send 3-5 high-quality reference images; some artists want a **photo of the body area**, especially for large or custom placements, "for flow, proportion, and sizing"; give placement exactly and size in inches or cm — [Minh Pham Tattoo, how to message an artist](https://minhphamtattoo.com/blog/how-to-message-a-tattoo-artist/); [Sortra quote checklist [SEO]](https://sortra.com/what-to-send-for-tattoo-price-quote/)
- Stating a budget lets the artist confirm feasibility right away and schedule a consultation if needed — [Hyperinkers](https://hyperinkers.com/blog/how-to-message-a-tattoo-artist); [Sortra [SEO]](https://sortra.com/what-info-to-include-when-booking-tattoo/)
- Vagaro intake forms can capture health disclosures, placement notes and design brief before the appointment — [Vagaro, tattoo booking form guide [vendor]](https://www.vagaro.com/learn/tattoo-booking-form)
- Templates exist on generic form builders (Jotform tattoo booking template) — [Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/form-templates/tattoo-booking-form)

### Inferences
- **Superset of fields** a preview tool should export with the mockup: name, contact, age 18+ confirmation; preferred artist; idea description; style; color vs black and grey (plus palette); exact placement (zone + side + orientation); **width x height in inches** (as it sits on the body, plus flat stencil size); reference images; photo(s) of the actual body area; cover-up / existing tattoos nearby (with photo); scars/stretch marks/skin notes; skin tone; first tattoo y/n; budget range; session-length preference; availability; and acknowledgement of the deposit policy.
- A 3D mockup **replaces the weakest field on current forms**, which is "approximate size", usually a client's guess. Pairing it with a real body-area photo still matters, because artists use that photo to judge flow and skin.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full real forms (egress blocked), so exact field labels and the number of form steps are unverified beyond the search summaries.
- No real form found in this search that explicitly has a "skin tone" field (only CTRL+ALT+Z's "skin type"); allergy questions appear on consent/waiver forms per vendor guides but no studio example was verified.

## 4. Booking/quote tools in use

### Takeaway
Tattoo-specific platforms (**Porter, Venue Ink, Tattoodo Books, Tattoo Studio Pro, InkBook, Bookedin, CoCreate.Ink, Apprentice**) are built around the tattoo flow: **request/intake form, then artist approval and quote, then deposit, then scheduling**. Generic salon tools (**Vagaro, Square Appointments, Booksy**) handle deposits and calendars but are weaker on design intake. Many solo artists still run on **Instagram DMs + Google Forms/Jotform**. None of the tools found offers a built-in **3D/body mockup**; design approval and mockups happen outside them, typically in Procreate.

### Cited Findings
- **Porter**: describes itself as the first software platform built exclusively for tattoo artists/studios; request-based flow "intake first, artist approval second, then scheduling"; custom forms (age, preferred artist, details, photo references); auto deposit collection with a configurable minimum deposit; collects waivers, consent forms and IDs before the booking; plans Artist Essentials $35/mo, Artist Pro $65/mo (adds marketing, consent forms, SMS), Studio Pro $200/mo (commission splits, tax, 1099s) — [Porter](https://www.getporter.io/); [Porter online booking [vendor]](https://www.getporter.io/features/online-booking); [Porter scheduling apps blog [vendor]](https://www.getporter.io/blog/best-scheduling-apps-for-tattoo-artists)

## 5. How size is communicated today and what goes wrong

### Takeaway
Today size is communicated by **client guesses in inches on the form**, then **Procreate mockups on a photo of the client's body**, then **printed stencils placed on the skin on the day** (often re-sized at the appointment). Common failures: **clients underestimate needed size** (artists routinely suggest going bigger), tiny detailed designs that **blur over time**, and **distortion on curved/wrapping areas** that a flat paper print doesn't show.

### Cited Findings
- Procreate mockups: artists photograph the client's body area, import it into Procreate, and position the design on the photo to check size and color before tattooing — [Envato Tuts+, Procreate for tattoos](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-use-procreate-for-tattoos--cms-109190); [10 Masters](https://www.10masters.com/en/blog/design-tattoos-with-procreate/); [Worldwide Tattoo Supply](https://worldwidetattoo.com/blogs/news/getting-started-with-procreate-a-beginners-guide-for-tattoo-artists-on-canvas-selection)
- Stencil workflow: Procreate canvas set to stencil-paper size (8.5 x 11 in, 300 DPI) so designs print at true scale — [Worldwide Tattoo Supply](https://worldwidetattoo.com/blogs/news/getting-started-with-procreate-a-beginners-guide-for-tattoo-artists-on-canvas-selection); [GridPrint, Procreate to print at exact size](https://gridprint.app/en/blog/procreate-tattoo-design-to-print)
- Artists usually measure in inches using the stencil or drawing before the session and place the artwork on the body against the available space, accounting for **body curvature, muscle shape and flow with movement** — [Sortra, size in inches [SEO]](https://sortra.com/tattoo-size-in-inches-explained/)
- Clients are advised to print reference art at 100% scale (no "fit to page") to test size, because a design that looks small on paper is often the real size it will be on the arm — [Tattooswizard size guide [SEO]](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/tattoo-size-guide)
- Size changes at the appointment are common; there is a guide specifically on changing size on the day — [Sortra [SEO]](https://sortra.com/can-you-change-size-at-tattoo-appointment/)
- Underestimation: first-time clients often underestimate how much space a design needs on skin, and artists often suggest going larger so the result stays readable; the advice reflects technical limits, skin behavior and aging, not upselling — [Sortra, why artists suggest bigger [SEO]](https://sortra.com/why-tattoo-artists-suggest-bigger-size/); [Sortra, first tattoo size [SEO]](https://sortra.com/how-to-choose-first-tattoo-size/)
- One guide claims clients underestimate ideal size by 20-40% and artists recommend sizes 30-50% larger than first requested. **Unverified, no methodology given; treat as anecdotal** — [Tattooswizard [SEO]](https://tattooswizard.com/blog/tattoo-size-guide)
- Studios refusing tiny fine-line work: Visions Tattoo Gallery published a post on why its artists "won't do them too small" — [Visions Tattoo Gallery](https://www.visionstattoogallery.com/blog/the-fine-linethe-fineline-tattoo-craze-why-visions-tattoo-artists-wont-do-them-too-small)
- Ink migration: all tattoos spread somewhat; with ultra-fine lines a crisp tattoo can blur or become illegible within a few years — [Vatican Tattoo Delray, why micro tattoos blur](https://vaticantattoodelray.com/why-micro-mini-tattoos-blur-over-time-the-real-science/)

### Inferences
- The clearest gaps a 3D tool fills: (a) **wrap/curvature**, since a flat Procreate photo mockup can't show how a 4 in wide design wraps around a forearm or how it moves on the ribs; (b) **true-scale size for the client** before booking, which cuts down on "change size on the day"; (c) **an objective W x H** (and wrap/arc length) to put on the request form instead of a guess.
- Useful tool features: a real-world ruler/grid overlay, readouts of flat stencil size vs on-body arc size, a warning when detail or text is below legibility thresholds at the chosen size, and side-by-side "your size vs artist-recommended size".

### Gaps
- No first-hand artist forum evidence (Reddit blocked) on how often quotes change because of size disputes; the underestimation percentages come only from SEO content.
- No source found on artists using AR or 3D tools for size communication (competitor coverage is outside this researcher's scope).

## 6. Minimum size rules of thumb (detail and lettering longevity)

### Takeaway
Rough rules: **script/lettering needs about 3-4 mm x-height/cap height or more** (smaller script tends to blur within a few years in high-motion areas), **light strokes should heal to at least about 0.3-0.4 mm**, letter spacing should be slightly open, and **very simple symbols or single words start around 1-2 in**. Expect lines to spread roughly 10-20% in year one and soften further over 5-15 years. These numbers come from blog/SEO content, not controlled studies.

### Cited Findings
- Script under about 3 mm x-height tends to heal fuzzy in high-motion spots within a few years; readable at arm's length with x-height 2.8-4.0 mm, lightest strokes healing to at least 0.3-0.4 mm, and slightly opened letter spacing — [AI for Tattoo, script minimum size guide [SEO]](https://www.aifortattoo.com/blog/script-tattoo-fonts-minimum-size-line-weight-legibility-guide)
- Avoid tiny script under about 3-4 mm cap height for good aging — [Sortra, lettering size [SEO]](https://sortra.com/tattoo-size-for-lettering-tattoos/)
